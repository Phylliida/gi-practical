## 1. Theorem A

**Count.** $|G|=2^k\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(2^k-2^i)$, $|B|=2^{k(k-1)/2+k}$, $[G:B]=[k]!$, the number of complete
flags. Exact integer check for $k\le64$. The constant $[k]!/2^{k(k+1)/2}\to0.28879$ matches the memo.

**Stabilizer and translations.** The right object is the flag of *directions*: $G$ acts on linear complete
flags through its linear part, with kernel $T$. The stabilizer in $\mathrm{GL}(k,2)$ of the standard flag is exactly
$U$ (at $q=2$ the torus is trivial), so the stabilizer in $G$ is $T\rtimes U=B$, a 2-group. Checked by enumeration for
$k\le4$: stabilizer equals $U$, $B$ is closed, contains $T$, every element has 2-power order, and it is generated by the
$k+\binom k2$ obvious elements. If one instead pinned the literal linear flag (through 0) the stabilizer would be $U$ alone
and the count would pick up a factor $2^k$; the memo's choice avoids that.

**Canonicity.** $(f\circ h)\circ s=f\circ(hs)$ and $hs=s'b$, so $h$ permutes the left cosets and
$\{\mathrm{Can}_B(f\circ h\circ s)\}_s=\{\mathrm{Can}_B(f\circ s')\}_{s'}$. Also $\mathrm{Can}_B(f\circ s)$ depends only on the coset $sB$, so the
output does not depend on the transversal. $\mathrm{Can}(f)$ lies in the orbit of $f$. **Sound.** The set of canonizers is the union over minimizing $s$
of $s\cdot(\text{canonizer coset})$, which is a right coset $\mathrm{Aut}(f)c$; hence $\mathrm{Iso}(f,g)=\mathrm{Aut}(f)\,c_fc_g^{-1}$.

**What the memo did not exercise.** Its script uses a brute-force lexmin over $B$, so the claimed
$\mathrm{poly}(N)$ canonizer was never run. I wrote it (`canon_rec`): a Luks-style recursion on the binary tree of cosets of $V_i$, which are
intervals in the integer order, so that order is block-compatible. It returns the true lexmin over $B$ and the
exact coset of minimizers on every string tested for $k\le4$ against enumeration. Worst case observed is the constant string, with exactly
$(4N^2-1)/3$ recursive calls.

Checked with this canonizer: all 256 functions at $k=3$ (invariant under all 1344 maps, $\mathrm{Can}(f)$ in the orbit, 10 classes = 10 orbits), and sampled
functions and maps at $k=4$. I rely on Babai–Luks only for the general statement; for this $B$ the recursion is explicit.

## 2. Theorem B

### 2.1 Factorization and sizes

$B\le H\le G$ with $T\le H$, $G=\bigsqcup sH$, $H=\bigsqcup Bt$, so $h=sbt$ uniquely. Exact: $|S_1|=\binom km_2$, $|S_2|=[m]!\,[k-m]!$, $|S_1||S_2|=[G:B]$.
For all $k\le64$: $|S_1|\le3.47\cdot2^{m(k-m)}$, $|S_2|\le2^{k^2/4+k/2}$ (odd $k$ included: there $|S_2|\approx0.083\cdot 2^{(k+1)^2/4}$, and $0.083\cdot2^{1/4}<1$), $|S_1|\le3.47\cdot2^{k^2/4}$.
The constant $|S_2|/2^{m^2+m}\to0.0834$ at $k=2m$ matches.

### 2.2 Collision

$f\circ(sbt)=g \iff (f\circ s)\circ b=g\circ t^{-1}$, which is exactly "same $B$-orbit". Forward and backward are both one line. **Every isomorphism yields a collision and every collision yields an isomorphism** $sbt$ with $b=\rho\rho'^{-1}$ from the two canonizers.

### 2.3 Output: the full coset

The memo's argument is right. With $z=sbt\in\mathrm{Aut}(f)$ in a bucket with fixed $s_0,t_0$, and $b':=b_{s,t_0}b_{s_0,t_0}^{-1}b_{s_0,t}$,
$$z\,(x_sx_{s_0}^{-1}y_t)^{-1}=s\,bb'^{-1}s^{-1},$$
which lies in $\mathrm{Aut}(f)$, hence $bb'^{-1}\in\mathrm{Aut}_B(f\circ s)$. Number of group elements is $|S_1|+|S_2|$ plus $\mathrm{poly}(N)$ per $s$. Space stays $|S_1|\mathrm{poly}(N)$: store the buckets on the $S_1$ side, stream the $S_2$ side, keep the Schreier–Sims chain.

**Implementation caveat** (an error I made): the recursion returns $\mathrm{Aut}_B$ of the canonical *word*; $\mathrm{Aut}_B(f\circ s)$ is its conjugate by the canonizer. The memo's formula is stated for $\mathrm{Aut}_B(f\circ s)$ and is correct.

Checked with the polynomial canonizer:

* $k=3$: factorization by exhaustion; collision = isomorphism on all $65536$ pairs; the generator set yields exactly $\mathrm{Aut}(f)$ for all 256 functions; $\mathrm{Iso}=\mathrm{Aut}(f)h$ on 200 planted pairs.
* $k=4$: $|\mathrm{Aut}|$ for constant, point, hyperplane, bent $x_1x_2+x_3x_4$, $x_1x_2x_3$ and a 3-colouring agrees with brute force (322560, 20160, 10752, 720, 2688, 768); decisions agree with brute force over all of $G$, including same-weight near-misses.
* $k=5$ ($|S_1|=155$, $|S_2|=63$): planted positives and negatives certified by a differing multiset of $|$Walsh coefficients$|$; closed-form $|\mathrm{Aut}|$ for constant, point and hyperplane ($319979520$, $9999360$, $5160960$).
* $k=6$ ($|S_1|=1395$, $|S_2|=441$): one planted positive and one certified negative.

### 2.4 A cheap improvement: use more blocks

The proof needs only $B\le H\le G$. For a parabolic with block sizes $(k_1,\dots,k_r)$, $|S_1|\approx 2^{(k^2-\sum k_i^2)/2}$ and $|S_2|\approx2^{(\sum k_i^2+k)/2}$. Two blocks can never balance these, since $|S_2|/|S_1|\ge2^{k/2}$ up to constants. Three blocks can: $\sum k_i^2\approx(k^2-k)/2$. Exact computation for $k\le64$:

| max blocks allowed | worst $\log_2\bigl(\max(|S_1|,|S_2|)/\sqrt{\#\text{flags}}\bigr)$ |
|---|---|
| 2 | 13.31 bits ($k=63$) |
| 3 | 3.07 bits ($k=54$) |
| 4 | 1.51 bits ($k=17$) |

Example $k=40$: two blocks $2^{416.42}$; $(1,16,23)$ gives $2^{409.79}$; $\sqrt{\#\text{flags}}=2^{409.10}$. Verified at $k=4$ with $(2,1,1)$ against brute force. **Cost:** space rises to about the time, so the memo's $2^{k^2/4}$ space is specific to the two-block split. In terms of $T\cdot\mathrm{poly}(N)$ this changes nothing; it matters only for Prop. B′'s query count.

### 2.5 Exponent arithmetic

Correct. The doc's $N^{(1/4)\log_2N+O(1)}$ form is the meaningful one. Do not read significance into $k/2$ or $k/4$ terms beside an unspecified $\mathrm{poly}(N)$.

"General form" sentence (M §2): "$|G|^{1/4+o(1)}$ for Lie type in natural characteristic" holds **only as rank $\to\infty$**. For $\mathrm{PGL}(2,q)$ the only parabolic is the Borel and the best is $|G|^{1/3}$. State the $o(1)$ as a function of rank.

### 2.6 Novelty

**Theorem A:** folklore. It is Luks's coset reduction with a 2-group at the bottom.

**Theorem B:** not found stated for truth tables or for AGL string isomorphism. Technique: exactly in print.
* Ros §2.1 (PDF pp.3–4) is the game with $f(N)$ top-of-tree and $g(N)$ bottom-of-tree paths. Taking the chain $B\le H\le G$ as the tree gives Theorem B's two sides.
* Ros §6 (pp.14–15) enumerates on both sides, computes **canonical forms in a tractable subclass, and sorts for a collision**: the same leaf comparison.
* LGR p.6 states the group version outright: at most $n^{(1/2)\log_pn+O(1)}$ composition series, canonical forms for composition series (Luks's fixed-composition-series work; LGR describe the canonical-form part as forthcoming), collision, giving $n^{(1/4)\log_pn+O(1)}$.
* The match is literal. **The composition series of the group $\mathbb F_2^k$ are precisely the complete flags**, so flags $\leftrightarrow$ composition series is not an analogy but a specialization of the counting. M §1 cites Rosenbaum for the analogy but not LGR p.6, the closest statement. Add it.

One difference favours the memo: LGR's route needs canonical forms of composition series, reported there as not yet written, while Theorem B needs only Babai–Luks for a 2-group, which is classical.

I would describe Theorem B as "the Rosenbaum/Le Gall–Rosenbaum template instantiated for AGL string isomorphism, apparently not written down", not as a new result. Absence: eight web searches (flags/Borel/Luks for Boolean functions; PSL/Sylow/collision; code equivalence parabolic meet-in-the-middle; classical-group natural action; 4-TI; upper-triangular double cosets; two citation checks) found nothing closer. Hedged accordingly.

**M §1's "False as a statement about the literature"** is misworded. The campaign note (moonshot-theory L434) reports that *we found* no improvement; the memo's own finding is that the literature does not state one either. Better: "the campaign's note is out of date: generic techniques already give exponents 1/2 and 1/4".

## 3. Proposition B′

**The proof is valid after small repairs:**

1. Conditioning: pairs are not independent of the past. Conditional on no collision so far, $h$ is uniform off a set of at most $T^2$ cosets of density $1/\#\text{flags}$, so each new pair collides with probability $\le1/(\#\text{flags}-T^2)$. Standard.
2. Labels must come from a space of size $\gg T^2$ (or be disjoint across the two cases), or the non-isomorphic case has spurious collisions.
3. Only the linear part of $h$ acts on flags. Harmless; say it. (Checked: $v_{f\circ h}(F)=v_f(h\cdot F)$ for real $\mathrm{Can}_B$ labels, $k=3,4$, translations included.)

**Stronger than stated.** The proof gives $\Omega(\sqrt{\#\text{flags}})=\Omega(2^{k^2/4+k/4})$, while the memo rounds $\#\text{flags}$ to $2^{k^2/2}$. "Tight" against Theorem B as written leaves a gap of $2^{k/4}$ in queries; §2.4 closes it to a small constant factor (at most 1.51 bits on $k\le64$).

**Is the model meaningful?** It is consistent: if $\mathrm{Aut}(f)=1$ the labels are injective on flags ($f\circ sbs'^{-1}=f$ forces $s'=sb$), so a rigid $f$ with its labels hidden behind a random relabelling realizes the oracle. It is also weak. It is the generic birthday bound for an equality oracle on a transitive $G$-set; nothing about AGL, flags or Borel groups enters except $\#\text{flags}$.

**What it does not show.** The opaque-handle assumption is carrying all the weight. A real $\mathrm{Can}_B(f\circ s)$ is a string that determines $f\circ s$ up to $B$, so a single query reveals the entire instance up to a known conjugate of $B$. The model does not restrict which flags are queried so much as forbid reading the answers. So:

* M §2 "beating Theorem B needs an algorithm that does not factor through flag-Borel canonical forms" is true **only if "factor through" means "uses the labels solely for equality tests"**.
* "The flag method has hit its limit" and Lead 5's "any improvement must use more than flag-indexed Borel canonical forms" overread it. Flag algorithms that prune using label *content* or invariants are outside the model. The memo's own §4.2 nondegenerate case ($2^{k^2/8}$) and Prop. D are of this kind.

**Replacement sentence.** "In the equality-oracle model, $\Omega(\sqrt{\#\text{flags}})$ queries are necessary, so collision of opaque flag labels cannot beat exponent $1/4$."

## 4. Theorem C

**Sound.** $\star$ occurs only at 0, so $A(0)=0$ and $A$ is linear. The colour (partial, $\{i\}$) is exactly $V_i\setminus0$, so $A(V_i)=V_i$ and $A=\bigoplus A_i$. Support is preserved, so full-support points go to full-support points, on which the colour is $T$. $T$ vanishes when some $x_i=0$, so nothing further is constrained. The pinned colours do force exactly $\prod\mathrm{GL}(k,2)$ and allow no extra maps. For $d=1$ the statement is trivially $\mathrm{GL}(k,2)$. $|\Sigma|=2^d+1$ is correct.

**Error: direction.** M §3 prints $\mathrm{Iso}(c_T,c_{T'})=\{(A_i):T'\circ(A_1,\dots,A_d)=T\}$. With the memo's own definition $\mathrm{Iso}(f,g)=\{A:f\circ A=g\}$, the right side must read $T\circ(A_1,\dots,A_d)=T'$. The printed set is $\mathrm{Iso}(c_{T'},c_T)$, the inverse set. Checked: they differ on 102 of the 256 pairs at $(d,k)=(2,2)$. Nothing downstream depends on it.

**Checked by machine.**
* $(d,k)=(2,2)$: of all 322560 elements of $\mathrm{AGL}(4,2)$, exactly the 36 direct sums preserve the pinned classes, and for all 256 pairs $(T,T')$ the solution set equals the explicit tensor stabilizer coset. A direct scan with no pinning shortcut agrees on 10 random pairs.
* $(d,k)=(2,3)$ and $(3,2)$, $K=6$: search over linear maps, pruned only by "$e_i$ goes to the same colour class", equals the explicit coset on planted and random instances.

**Corollaries.** C1 arithmetic is right. C2's $c/16$ is right. Three calibrations:

* "No $2^{o(k^2)}$" gives an infinitely-often statement, and only at $4\mid K$ without padding. Say so.
* Brute force for $d$-TI is $2^{(d-2)k^2}$, i.e. exponent $\frac{d-2}{d^2}K^2$, which peaks at $1/8$ at $d=4$. So this reduction **cannot certify more than $K^2/8$** even if brute force were optimal for every $d$, while Theorem B is at $K^2/4$. "$2^{\Theta(K^2)}$" is right; the constants never meet.
* "For $d\ge4$ this is new" undersells: by the memo's own §1 and GIQS pp.12–13 (Sun and IMQSZ "do not work for tensors over $\mathbb F_2$", said of alternating 3-tensors), the $d=3$ case over $\mathbb F_2$ is not visibly covered either. I did not establish the state of general 3-TI over $\mathbb F_2$.

Novelty: a one-line gadget. The GI version is already at moonshot-theory L437. Not found in print for AGL.

Remark M §3(a), reduction to two colours, is [SKETCH] and was not checked.

## 5. Effect on the campaign

**5a. "Prop. 2's bound holds for HJ as written, not for the problem."** Sound, immediately from Theorem A. It is not in conflict with anything: moonshot-theory L487 already says Prop. 2 is "a lower bound for one specific algorithm (HJ), not for a model".

**5b. Sylow row.** The count is right: for $\mathrm{AGL}(r,2)$, $|P|_{2'}=[r]!$, so $\log|P|_{2'}=(1/2+o(1))\log|P|$. Sylow subgroups are computable deterministically in polynomial time (Kantor; from memory). At the AGL root the claim reduces to Theorem A and is proven. At internal nodes it is a sketch with these gaps:

* **The row cost is not $\mu_{2'}$.** After $|P|_{2'}$ coset branches, each with no shrinkage, the 2-group chain costs two cosets and two halves per halving, $d^2$ children of size $n/d$, against $|P|d$ for enumeration. The bundled row has $\mu'=\log|P|_{2'}/\log d+2$. The memo's "$\mu\approx(r+1)/2$" drops the $+2$. Numerically for $\mathrm{AGL}(r,2)$: $\mu'/\mu=0.89,0.78,0.65,0.58,0.54,0.52$ at $r=4,6,12,24,48,96$. "Halved" is asymptotic only, and not close at $r=12$, i.e. $N=4096$.
* **The row needs bundling.** The Sylow step has zero domain shrink, so it must be charged together with the whole halving chain as a single composite row. The memo notes the row is "needed" but did not write it.
* **Prop. 2.1 needs a Sylow version.** The bound used is on $\log|P|/\log d'$. One must show $\log[P:S]\le(\lambda/2+O(1+\min\{\log w,\log\log d'\}))\log d'$ for a suitable solvable $S\le P$ across O'Nan–Scott types. I expect this holds, by type-checking the natural-module cases: $(r^2+r)/(2(r-1))$ for linear, about $\lambda/4$ for symplectic. It is not done. Note for affine $P$ with point stabilizer in cross characteristic one needs $V\rtimes\mathrm{Syl}_p$, not $\mathrm{Syl}_p(P)$.
* HJ itself was not read for this report; I am relying on the campaign's account of its dispatcher.

**5c. "Theorem R\*\* keeps its form with the $\lambda$ coefficient halved." Speculative as worded.**

R\*\* is $\exp(O(\log N(\log w+\lambda+1)))$ proved with a single $\Lambda:=C(\log w+\lambda+1)$ for all rows (W1 §3; the redteam noted the same at `redteam-w1-structure.md` L177). There is no separate coefficient on $\lambda$. Inside the $O(\cdot)$ the claim is vacuous; outside it is unproved. Prop. 2.1 is itself a [SKETCH], its absolute $C$ is untracked, and its Type II line is written as $\mu\le2r+O(1)$. R\*\* is further conditional on (H0).

**Statement I would accept** in place of it, labelled [SKETCH]: "If the composite row is admissible, the (E1) $\beta$-part of Cor. 3.1 becomes $\log|P|_{p'}/\log d'+O(1)\approx r/2+O(1)$ for natural-module classical $P$, an improvement in that row's constant only. Whether R\*\*'s overall constant improves depends on untracked constants."

Row G in M §0 and the §7 bullet should be relabelled accordingly. The claim about the $\mathrm{AGL}(k,2)$ root ($N^{(\log N)/2+O(1)}$) may stay, since it is Theorem A.

## 6. Subclass claims

**6a. Vectorial quadratics.** Citation is accurate. GIQS Thm 1.2 is the *space* version and Thm 1.3 the *tuple* version, $f_i^S=g_i$ for all $i$, in $2^{O((m+n)^{1.5})}$. Tuple is what $f\circ A=g$ needs. It is a decision statement; the memo says so. The reduction checks: enumerate the $2^k$ translations; compare constants; a degree-$\le2$ function with zero constant is uniquely a formal homogeneous quadratic ($x_i\leftrightarrow x_i^2$), equivariantly, so function equality is form equality. $m=o(k^{4/3})$ gives $2^{o(k^2)}$.

One normalization to add: the algorithm stated for Thm 1.3 (PDF p.30) takes **linearly independent** $f_i$ and $g_i$. Reduce to a basis and compare linear relations first. This also shows the right parameter is $\dim\mathrm{span}(f_i)$ rather than $m$. GIQS is a five-week-old unrefereed preprint; I read statements only. The memo discloses this. The quoted "do not work for tensors over $\mathbb F_2$" is verbatim (p.13).

**6b. Random $g$.** Prop. D is correct. Pairs in different $U$-cosets match with probability $2^{-2^t}$; pairs in one coset with probability $2^{-2^{t-1}}$; both union bounds are right. $A$ is recovered uniquely from patterns because $P_{g,u}$ is injective. Nits: "$\mathrm{sep}\le\log_2k+3$" should be $\lceil\log_2k\rceil+3$; for general $g$ a separating frame must be searched, which costs the same $2^{(\mathrm{sep}+1)k}$. Measured smallest separating $t$ on 5 random $g$ each was 4–5 at $k=8$, 5 at $k=10,12$, 5–6 at $k=14$, against the memo's 6–7. The bound is fine as an upper bound; the memo's §6 data (discrete at the root) suggests random $g$ is much easier than $2^{k\log k}$, which I did not prove.

## 8. The strongest statement I would sign

Let $V=\mathbb F_2^k$, $N=2^k$, and fix the block-compatible canonizer for $B$ (Babai–Luks; explicit recursion checked here).

1. **(A)** Canonical forms and $\mathrm{Iso}$ under $\mathrm{AGL}(k,2)$, deterministically in $[k]!\cdot\mathrm{poly}(N)=2^{k^2/2}\mathrm{poly}(N)$.
2. **(B)** Deterministically, in time $(\binom{k}{m}_2+[m]![k-m]!)\mathrm{poly}(N)=2^{k^2/4}\mathrm{poly}(N)$ and space $\binom{k}{m}_2\mathrm{poly}(N)$, $m=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$: decide $f\cong g$ and output a witness $h$ and generators of $\mathrm{Aut}(f)$, so $\mathrm{Iso}(f,g)=\mathrm{Aut}(f)h$. Any parabolic $H\ge B$ gives time $([G:H]+[H:B])\mathrm{poly}(N)$. This is an instance of the Rosenbaum and Le Gall–Rosenbaum template.
3. **(B′)** For algorithms with equality-only access to opaque labels $v_f,v_g$ with $v_g(F)=v_f(hF)$, deciding existence of $h$ with constant advantage needs $\Omega(\sqrt{[k]!})$ queries. Nothing is claimed about algorithms reading the labels.
4. **(C)** $\{A\in\mathrm{AGL}(dk,2):c_T\circ A=c_{T'}\}=\{(A_1,\dots,A_d)\in\prod\mathrm{GL}(k,2):T\circ(A_1,\dots,A_d)=T'\}$. Hence $2^{o(K^2)}$ AGL-SI implies $2^{o(k^2)}$ $d$-TI over $\mathbb F_2$ for each fixed $d$.
5. **(E)** Mod GIQS Thm 1.3 (unrefereed): deciding equivalence of $\mathbb F_2^k\to\mathbb F_2^m$ degree-$\le2$ colourings in $2^k\cdot2^{O((m'+k)^{1.5})}$, $m'$ the span dimension.
6. **(D)** For uniform $g$, whp $\mathrm{Iso}(f,g)$ is computed in $2^{k\lceil\log_2k\rceil+O(k)}\mathrm{poly}(N)$ for every $f$.
7. **(Campaign)** Prop. 2 is a statement about HJ's dispatcher only. At the $\mathrm{AGL}(k,2)$ root a Sylow-descent dispatcher does $N^{(\log N)/2+O(1)}$ work. No statement about Theorem R\*\*'s constants is supported.

The $k=5$ and $k=6$ runs have no brute-force ground truth: positives are planted and negatives certified by $|$Walsh$|$ multisets, so a false "isomorphic" on an unrelated pair would only be caught if it were one of those. The memo's `bidirectional_check.py` was rerun unchanged and passes (16 s).

Two first-run assertion failures were bugs in the referee script, not in the memo, and are recorded because the first is an easy trap for anyone implementing Theorem B: (1) generators of $\mathrm{Aut}_B$ of the canonical word were used without conjugating by the canonizer; (2) a "certified negative" compared signed Walsh multisets, which are not affine-invariant since translations flip signs; the invariant is the multiset of $|W|$.
