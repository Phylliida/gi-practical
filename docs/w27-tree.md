## 1. Setting

Notation of W8. $V=V'=[b]^m$. Rows $X=V\times[b]$, columns $Y=V'\times[b]$, $N=|X||Y|=b^{2m+2}$.
**HT2** is SI under $G_1\times G_2$, $G_i=K_i\rtimes T_i$, with $T_i=S_b^m$ in product action on $V$ and
$K_i=S_b^{V}$ the *gauge* group (one $S_b$ per block). This is $S_b^{(P)}$ for
$P=(A_m{<}k)\sqcup(A_m{<}k)$. An instance is a block matrix $M_{v,v'}\in\Sigma^{b\times b}$.

For a fixed top element the problem is a coset CSP with variables in $S_b$, FPT (ADKT). For fixed gauges
it is SI over an antichain, FPT (BCQ/ADKT). Theorem 1 of W8 gives $2^{O(b^{m+1})}$, because the group
$G_2$ shared across the left fibres has orbits of size $b^{m+1}$.

**Reduction 0** **[PROVEN]**. With $T_2$ fixed (or with $G_2$ replaced by $K_2\rtimes C$ for enumerable $C$),
Theorem 1's argument is FPT: processing $G_1$ first leaves a shared group with orbits $\le b$ on a faithful
domain ($K_2$), plus the small domain of the remaining coordinates. So the whole difficulty is the
semidirect product $K_2\rtimes T_2$ when both factors are large.

## 2. Height-2 posets

### 2.1 Prop N: the depth barrier **[PROVEN]**

Let $Y_1=A_5$ and $Y_{c+1}=A_5\wr Y_c$ in the natural imprimitive action, on $5^{c}$ points. Let $B_j\trianglelefteq Y_c$ be
the kernel of the action on the top $j$ levels, so $B_0=Y_c$, $B_c=1$, $B_j/B_{j+1}\cong A_5^{5^{j}}$, and
$B_j/B_{j'}\cong Y_{j'-j}^{\,5^{j}}$ for $j<j'$.

Non-soluble length is not usable here: $\lambda$ is not subgroup-monotone, since $A_n$ contains deep
wreath towers while $\lambda(A_n)=1$. The tower $Y_c$ as a *section* is monotone by construction.

*Fact.* The normal subgroups of $Y_c$ are exactly the $B_j$. Let $1\ne L\trianglelefteq Y_c$ and $B=B_{c-1}$. If $L\cap B=1$
then $[L,B]=1$; but $C_{Y_c}(B)=1$, since an element moving a factor does not centralise $B$ and a base element with a
non-trivial coordinate does not centralise that factor. So $L\cap B\ne1$. $B$ is minimal normal
(the commutator with one coordinate, then transitivity of the top on the factors), so $L\ge B$. Induct on $Y_c/B\cong Y_{c-1}$.

**Lemma 1.** If $Y_c$ is a quotient of a subgroup of a direct product of copies of $S_b$, then
$c\le D(b):=1+\log_5\log_{60}b!$.

*Proof.* Let $\varphi:H\to Y_c$ be onto with $H\le\prod_{j\le r}S_b$. Put $H_j=H\cap\ker(\text{first }j\text{ projections})$.
The groups $\varphi(H_j)$ are normal, so they form a chain among the $B_i$. Take the step where $\varphi(H_j)\ge B_{c-1}$ but
$\varphi(H_{j+1})\not\ge B_{c-1}$; since the normal subgroups are a chain, $\varphi(H_{j+1})=1$. So $B_{c-1}=A_5^{5^{c-1}}$ is a section
of $H_j/H_{j+1}\le S_b$, which gives $60^{5^{c-1}}\le b!$. ∎

**Lemma 2.** Let $1=G_0\le\dots\le G_\ell=G$ be a normal series with each $G_i/G_{i-1}$ embedded in a direct
product of copies of $S_b$. If $Y_c$ is a section of $G$, then $c\le\ell D(b)$.

*Proof.* Let $\varphi:H\to Y_c$ be onto, $H\le G$. The $\varphi(H\cap G_i)$ are a chain $B_{j(i)}$ with $j(0)=c$, $j(\ell)=0$. The step $i$ quotient
$B_{j(i)}/B_{j(i-1)}\cong Y_{j(i-1)-j(i)}^{\,5^{j(i)}}$ is a quotient of $(H\cap G_i)/(H\cap G_{i-1})$; project to one factor and apply Lemma 1. ∎

**Prop N.** Let $P$ have height $\ell$, $G_k\le S_b$. Then every section $Y_c$ of $\prod^{(P)}G_k$ has $c\le\ell D(b)$.

*Proof.* Let $N_i$ be the kernel of the action on the down-set of elements of rank $\le i$. Then $N_i/N_{i+1}$ is the
pointwise product of the functions $\pi_k$ for $k$ of rank $i+1$, a direct product of copies of $G_k\le S_b$. Apply Lemma 2. ∎

**Definition.** A many-one reduction $\varphi$ between isomorphism problems is **Aut-reflecting** if $\mathrm{Aut}(x)$ is isomorphic
to a section of $\mathrm{Aut}(\varphi(x))$ for every instance $x$. All reductions of T2 Theorem A and W8 Prop H are Aut-reflecting (there
$\mathrm{Aut}(\varphi(x))$ restricts onto $\mathrm{Aut}(x)$).

**Corollary N.1.** For $b_0\ge5$ there is no parameterized Aut-reflecting reduction from R1$_c(b_0)$ to SI under
$S_{b}^{(P)}$ with $P$ of height $\le\ell$, for any function $b=f(b_0)$ and any fixed $\ell$.

*Proof.* The identity tanglegram of depth $h$ has automorphism group $W_h(b_0)\ge Y_h$. Prop N would give $h\le\ell D(f(b_0))$. ∎

**Reading.** W8 §5.3 asked whether height-2 posets are FPT or R1$_c$-hard, and said a hardness answer would mean "$\mu$ is essentially
right". Hardness in the only sense the campaign has ever used is impossible, so the question reduces to the FPT side. W8 Prop H.2 used
families with induced $C_m\sqcup C_m$. The barrier says the height of the poset, not $\mu$, is what a hardness reduction has to spend. It does not
say which posets of *unbounded* height with no large $C_s\sqcup C_s$ are easy: for instance $C_m\sqcup(A_m{<}k)$ has one deep tower and
$\mu\approx m$, and I have no answer for it.

### 2.2 Lemma MD: no small faithful domain **[PROVEN]**

**Lemma MD.** Let $b\ge5$ and $W=\mathrm{Sym}(b)\wr R$ with $R$ transitive on $r$ points (so $W=W_h$ with $r=b^{h-1}$, or
$W=G_2$ with $r=b^m$). Then the minimal faithful permutation degree of $W$ is $rb$.

*Proof.* Let $M=\mathrm{Alt}(b)^r$. As in Prop N's fact, every non-trivial normal subgroup contains $M$, so a faithful action has an
orbit on which $M$ acts non-trivially, and that orbit is faithful. So we need the least $|W:H|$ with $M\not\le H$.
Write $K=H\cap M$, $e=|M:K|$, and $f'$ for the index of the image of $H$ in the top group; then $|W:H|\ge ef'$.
The $H$-orbits on the $r$ factors have size $\ge r/f'$.
* If $S=\{i:\pi_i(K)\ne\mathrm{Alt}(b)\}\neq\varnothing$: $S$ is $H$-invariant, so $|S|\ge r/f'$, and $e\ge b^{|S|}$ (proper subgroups of $\mathrm{Alt}(b)$ have index $\ge b$). Then, writing $y=r/f'$, $ef'\ge(r/y)\,b^{y}\ge rb$ for $y\ge1$, because $b^y/y$ is increasing there; if $f'>r$ then $|S|\ge1$ already gives $ef'>rb$.
* If $S=\varnothing$: $K$ is subdirect and proper, so by Scott's lemma a product of diagonals over a partition with a non-singleton class. The non-singleton classes cover an $H$-invariant set of size $\ge r/f'$, so $e\ge|\mathrm{Alt}(b)|^{r/(2f')}\ge b^{r/f'}$, the same bound (and if $f'>r$, a non-singleton class alone gives $e\ge|\mathrm{Alt}(b)|\ge b$).

The point stabiliser in the natural action attains $rb$. ∎

So ADKT or S-W on any faithful domain costs $2^{\Omega(rb)}$ for the shared group. Any FPT algorithm for HT2 must exploit the structure of the cosets it
intersects, not the group alone.

### 2.3 Theorem G: pinning **[PROVEN mod ADKT]**

Call a set $B$ of row blocks **pinning** for $x$ if for every column block $v'$, two columns of $v'$ either differ on $\bigcup B$ or are twins (identical on all rows).

**Theorem G.** If $x$ has a pinning set of $\rho$ row blocks, $\mathrm{Iso}_{G_1\times G_2}(x,y)$ is computable in time
$$(b!\,b^m)^{\rho}\cdot b!\,2^{O(b)}N^{O(1)}.$$
The same holds for a pinning set of column blocks.

*Proof.* Enumerate images of the ordered rows of $B$ in $y$: $\le(b!\,b^m)^\rho$ choices. Fix one. Each column $(v',c)$ of $x$ gets
$\phi_x(v',c)=$ its restriction to the rows of $B$ (a tuple, in the fixed row order); $y$'s columns get $\phi_y$ from the chosen images. If $y$ does not satisfy the
pinning condition for the chosen images, reject the branch: pinning is preserved by isomorphism. Define on $X\times V'$
$$\tilde x(r,v')=\bigl(\text{tag}(r),\ \{\!\{(\phi_x(v',c),\,x(r,v',c)):c\in[b]\}\!\}\bigr),$$
with $\text{tag}(r)$ the position of $r$ in $B$ (or none), and $\tilde y$ likewise.

*Claim:* $(g_1,t,\gamma)$ with $g_1\in G_1$, $t\in T_2$, $\gamma\in K_2$ is an isomorphism sending the chosen rows as prescribed iff $(g_1,t)$ is
an isomorphism $\tilde x\to\tilde y$ under $G_1\times T_2$, and $\gamma$ is then determined up to twin permutations. Forward is immediate.
Conversely, equality of multisets at $(r,v')$ gives, for each $\phi$, equal numbers of columns with that $\phi$. Columns of a block sharing $\phi$ are twins
in both instances, so any bijection between equal $\phi$-classes works for every row at once. That defines $\gamma_{v'}$.

SI under $G_1\times T_2$ is SI over the poset $(A_m{<}k)\sqcup A_m$ on $X\times V'$. The linear extension "left part first" has $\mu=0$ (each $R_t$ contains only
elements without unprocessed predecessors). W8 Theorem 1 applies: $b!\,2^{O(b)}N^{O(1)}$. ∎

**Theorem G+.** More generally, let $I$ be a set of $r$ left coordinates and $B$ a set of $\rho$ row blocks. If columns of each block are separated, modulo twins, by the
tuple ($\phi$ on $B$; for each $u\in[b]^I$ the histogram of $x(\cdot,s)$ over rows with $a|_I=u$), then the cost is
$(b!)^r(b!\,b^m)^\rho\cdot b!\,2^{O(b)}N^{O(1)}$. **[PROVEN mod ADKT]**

*Proof.* Enumerate the $r$ permutations; the histograms are invariant under elements of $G_1$ with those permutations (they permute the rows of each fibre). The rest is as above, with $G_1$ replaced by the coset fixing those coordinates; Theorem 1 handles any $G_i\le S_b$ after translating $y$. ∎

Instances covered: all "matched" instances where some block row separates each column block; instances where a coordinate-fibre histogram does (the example in §2.5 where each column block is tied to one top coordinate of the other side); generic strings. The theorem is elementary, and its role is to isolate what a hard height-2 instance must avoid.

### 2.4 Theorem C: a soluble surviving top **[PROVEN mod Luks]**

Let $\tau$ be any isomorphism-invariant string on $V\times V'$ (e.g. local block types, or their arity-3 refinement), and $C\le T_1\times T_2$ its automorphism group, computed in FPT time by W8 Theorem 1 (antichain case). Every isomorphism has its top part in the coset $Ct_0$. Let $r$ be the least size of a coordinate set $R$ such that the pointwise stabiliser of $R$ in $C$ is soluble.

**Theorem C.** SI under $(K_1\times K_2)\rtimes C$ runs in $(b!)^{r+2}N^{O(1)}$.

*Proof.* Enumerate the image of each coordinate in $R$. The remaining group $G'=K\rtimes C'$ has $C'$ soluble. Run Luks's recursion, taking block systems coarser than the $K$-orbit partition of $X\times Y$ first. On those, the primitive quotient is a section of $C'$, so $|P|\le\mathrm{poly}(m_P)$ on $m_P$ blocks and the recurrence telescopes (Luks 1982). Each remaining orbit lies inside one $K$-orbit, of size $\le b^2$, and is handled by the orbit rule additively at cost $(b!)^2$ each. ∎

Corollary: abelian or soluble surviving tops are FPT, however the gauges couple. So a hard height-2 instance needs a non-soluble surviving top on many coordinates, by Theorem C.

### 2.5 The open residue

What remains of HT2 after Theorems G, G+, C and Reduction 0: non-soluble surviving top on $\omega(1)$ coordinates, and no small pinning set. Take the cleanest case of the residue: every block is either constant, or a bijection between row and column gauges (a permutation-type block, $D_{v,v'}\cong S_b$ diagonal). The bijection blocks form a bipartite **gain graph** $\Gamma\subseteq V\times V'$ with labels in $S_b$, defined up to switching at vertices, and $C$ acts on it. If some row block is joined in $\Gamma$ to every column block (for instance $\Gamma$ complete bipartite), that block pins and Theorem G applies; a dominating set of $\rho$ row blocks pins with $\rho$ blocks. Otherwise the isomorphism test is: find $t\in C$ with the two gain graphs switching-equivalent after $t$.

**Why this is the right open object.**
* *Flat components are fine.* If a component has trivial holonomy, one frame fixes all frames in it, and $\{\text{gauge-fixed string for each frame choice}\}$ is a $T$-equivariant object of size $b!\,|Z|$. Objects of total size $s$ are isomorphism-tested under $T$ in $2^{O(b)}\mathrm{poly}(s)$ (S-W `theo:obj`; this is STR Theorem L's mechanism). **[SKETCH]**
* *Curvature is the obstacle.* With holonomy group $\mathrm{Hol}\ne1$, frames are canonical only modulo $\mathrm{Hol}$, and after reduction to $\mathrm{Hol}$ no local data remains. Complete invariants are values of a representation of $\pi_1(\Gamma)$ up to simultaneous conjugacy, and a $T$-equivariant generating family of closed walks is needed. With bounded per-coordinate support such families have size $N^{O(1)}$.
* *The relevant conjecture.* **[SPECULATION]** If $C$ has large point stabilisers on $V\sqcup V'$, then $\pi_1(\Gamma)$ is normally generated by closed walks using $O(1)$ values per coordinate (and $f(b)$ walks on $f(b)$ coordinates). The heuristic: an independent non-trivial stabiliser on two coordinates yields commuting squares, and regular coordinates can be enumerated (a regular part has $\le N$ elements). E2 supports this on orbitals of full product groups. With abelian coefficients this is exactly "$W^\perp$ generated by a small $C$-invariant set", the module-theoretic form of the same question: $t\in C$ with $x^t\equiv y$ mod $W$, $W=B^1(\Gamma)$. Over $F_p$ with $p\nmid|C|$ it is trivial (equivariant projection), and the modular case $p\le b$ is the real one.

**Where the attempts stopped.**

| route | outcome | stops at |
|---|---|---|
| CI directly in $G_2=K_2\rtimes T_2$ for arbitrary subgroups | project to $T_2$ (ADKT), then a double-coset condition $d(t)\in K_A\backslash K_2/K_B$ on $t\in C$ | the zero set of $d$ is a coset, but computing it is stabiliser-of-a-point on a set of size $2^{\Omega(b^m)}$; decomposes along fibres only if the coupling components do |
| row-first DP with definable cores (intersections of $\mathrm{Aut}$ of strings are free) | the $\exists$ over each row block's order (index $\le b!$ per block) compounds | $(b!)^{b^s}$ at level $s$: W2 Cor 2.3's regime |
| canonical gauge-fixing from a lexicographic order of $V$ | correct for each order | the orders are moved by $T$: $(b!)^m$ of them, so XP |
| recursing CI into HT2 at smaller $m$ (balanced left/right DP) | $T(m)\le(b!)^2T(m-1)+\mathrm{poly}$ | $b!$ per level: XP, the same multiplicative form as Prop S |
| reduction to a bounded-$\mu$ poset by re-coordinatising gauges | none: $k'$ has all of $A_m$ below it in every linear extension | $\mu$ is intrinsic to the fibre order |

## 3. R1$_c$

### 3.1 No definable one-call combine **[PROVEN mod T2 Thm B's encoding]**; E3

W6 §2.3 showed the recurrence $C(n)\le\sum_iC(n_i)+C(n-n_{\max})$ is polynomial, and that FPT would follow from a combine making **one** call on $\le n-n_{\max}$ leaves. W6 lead 2 asked for $\rho_i(G^{\rm loc}(u_i))$ to be defined by a tanglegram on the frontier plus $O(|S(u)|)$ auxiliary leaves. This fails.

**Model DC (definable one-call combine).** At a node $u$ with children $u_1,\dots,u_k$ and frontier $F=\mathrm{bottom}(S(u))$, the combine outputs one coloured tanglegram $I$ on $\ell$ leaves and a map $\iota:F\to\mathrm{nodes}(I)$ such that the restriction of $\mathrm{Aut}(I)$ to $\iota(F)$, read on $F$, equals $\bigcap_i\rho_i(G^{\rm loc}(u_i))$. Post-processing by arbitrary group computation is *not* part of the model.

**Lemma 3.1 (realisation).** For every $s$ and every code $C\le F_2^s$ there is a tanglegram of out-degree 2 at a node $u$ with two children such that $|L(u_2)|=2s$, $|L(u_1)|=O(s^2)$, $F$ consists of $s$ swappable pairs, $\rho_2$ is all of $F_2^s$, and $\rho_1=C$.

*Proof.* Take a parity-check matrix of $C$ with at most $s$ rows. Replace each check of weight $w$ by a chain of $w-2$ checks of weight $\le3$ on auxiliary variables; each auxiliary variable occurs in exactly two checks and is determined by the data bits. This is the multipede form: a data variable may occur in many checks, which is allowed in T2 §5.1's tree 2 (a variable pair $w^0,w^1$ over all its incidences, grouped by check under a rigid comb). The T2 Thm B encoding, with this generalisation, gives child $u_1$ on $O(s^2)$ leaves whose automorphisms are exactly the flips by words of $C$ on data pairs, each extended uniquely to the auxiliaries. Add to the node $q_j^i$ of $T'$ (the pair's side $i$) one extra leaf $\ell_{j,i}\in L_2$, with $T_{u_2}$ a rigid comb of the cherries $\{\ell_{j,0},\ell_{j,1}\}$. ∎

**Prop 3.2.** In Model DC, $\ell=\Omega(s^2/\log s)$ on the instances of Lemma 3.1, whereas $n-n_{\max}=2s$. So no definable one-call combine with $\ell\le c\,(n-n_{\max})$ exists, for any constant $c$, already at $b=2$.

*Proof.* Fixing $k=s/2$, the number of codes is $\ge2^{k(s-k)}=2^{s^2/4}$. The number of pairs $(I,\iota)$ is at most $2^{O(\ell\log\ell)}\cdot(4\ell)^{2s}$: two rooted trees on $\ell$ leaves, a colouring by $\le\ell$ colours (node colours can be pushed to leaves), and the map $\iota$. The output must determine $C$, since $\rho_2$ is full. Comparing gives the bound. ∎

*What it does and does not say.* It rules out exactly W6 lead 2: staying inside R1 with a linear-size call. The problem at $b=2$ is polynomial anyway (Luks), so this is a limit on the route and not a hardness statement. A combine that keeps generators of $\rho_1$ and asks for a coset intersection on the frontier is outside the model and is what W6 Prop S.4 already produces; TreeCI on a small domain is the unavoidable shape. The bound shows why (R2)-style definability has to allow super-linear auxiliary size: the frontier has $2^{\Theta(s^2)}$ possible behaviours and $s$ leaves cannot carry them.

## 4. Lower-bound side, collected

1. **Corollary N.1** **[PROVEN]**: no Aut-reflecting parameterized reduction from R1$_c$ to any bounded-height poset family.
2. **Lemma MD consequence** **[PROVEN]**: any algorithm whose combine is coset intersection on a faithful domain, with a cost of $2^{O(\text{orbit})}$, pays $2^{\Omega(b^{m+1})}$ on the shared group of HT2 and $2^{\Omega(N)}$ on $W_h$.
3. **Prop 3.2** **[PROVEN mod T2 Thm B's encoding]**: no linear-size definable one-call combine.
4. **Not achieved.** A lower bound for coset-splitting with memoisation, or a prover–delayer argument on twisted coordinate tanglegrams. FPT §3.4's model is still open. The obstacle I met: pruning kills branches on instances whose branches are distinct, and memoising by isomorphism type merges them on instances where they survive, so a lower bound has to exhibit surviving, non-isomorphic branches, and I did not construct such a family.

## 6. Novelty

Searches (2026-11-09): string isomorphism or coset intersection over generalized wreath products or posets with FPT bounds; non-soluble length and its monotonicity; minimal degree of iterated wreath products. They are thin, and the claims below should be read with that in mind.

* **Prop N / Cor N.1.** The definition of non-soluble length is Khukhro–Shumyatsky ([arXiv:1310.2434](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.2434)); the invariant used here is the section depth of the $A_5$-tower, chosen because $\lambda$ itself is not subgroup-monotone. The argument is elementary; I found no statement of it. The Aut-reflecting framing is mine.
* **Lemma MD.** Minimal degrees of wreath products are classical (Easdown–Praeger, [Bull. Austral. Math. Soc. 1988](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/0C55A2C207214D2BFE4768F1D11B9EA0/S0004972700027489a.pdf/on-minimal-faithful-permutation-representations-of-finite-groups.pdf); more recently Alotaibi–Easdown, [preprint 2023](https://talus.maths.usyd.edu.au/u/pubs/publist/preprints/2023/alotaibi-10.pdf)). I did not find this exact formula for $\mathrm{Sym}(b)\wr R$ with $b\ge5$ and $R$ transitive, and it is very likely known or folklore.
* **Theorems G, G+, C.** Elementary combinations of W8 Theorem 1, ADKT and Luks. Nothing similar was found for generalized wreath products over posets.
* **Prop 3.2.** The counting argument is elementary; the realisation step adapts T2 Thm B and the multipede gadgets of Neuen–Schweitzer.

Search hits for the first query included [GNS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.04659), [Neuen 2002.06997](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.06997) and Babai's quasipolynomial paper; none addresses posets or FPT over iterated wreath products.

## 7. Leads (ranked)

1. **The gain-graph core (§2.5).** Prove or refute: if $C\le S_b^{M}$ and a $C$-invariant graph $\Gamma$ on $[b]^M$ are given, then $\pi_1(\Gamma)$ is normally generated by closed walks with $O(1)$ values per coordinate, except on coordinates where $C$ is enumerable. A proof gives $T$-equivariant objects for curved gain graphs, and with Theorems G and C would very likely decide HT2 for permutation-type blocks. A counterexample family is a new candidate hard family at height 2.
2. **Partial couplings.** Extend §2.5 from permutation-type blocks to general Goursat data $D_{v,v'}$, by induction along a composition chain of $S_b$. Abelian sections give the module question; the modular case $p\le b$ needs the submodule structure of $\bigotimes F_p[b]$ for $C$.
3. **Posets with one deep tower.** $C_m\sqcup(A_m{<}k)$ has unbounded height but no $C_s\sqcup C_s$. Prop N does not apply. It is the next test of whether "two long incomparable chains" is the true boundary.
4. **A $C_s\sqcup C_s$-sensitive barrier.** Prop N sees height only. A sharper invariant might exclude Aut-reflecting reductions to every $C_s\sqcup C_s$-free family, which would make W8's dividing line exact.
5. **Non-definable one-call combines.** Prop 3.2 leaves room for one call plus generator-given post-processing. That is TreeCI on the frontier: the (R2)/definability question, in sharper form.
6. **Prover–delayer.** §4 item 4 states what such an argument must supply: a family with many surviving, pairwise non-isomorphic branches.
