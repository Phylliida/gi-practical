# Bounded degree: Luks's reduction and the dispatcher modification (M)

This round implements the algorithmic side of moonshot-theory.md §4.1 (Theorem R and its
corollary). There are two parts:

* `gi/bounded.py`: Luks's reduction of GI for graphs of maximum degree d to string isomorphism
  under groups whose composition factors embed in Sym(d − 1). Each layer is solved by
  `Solver.solve`.
* `Solver(cameron_no_enum=True)`: the modification (M). A primitive quotient that is recognized as
  a Cameron group with m > q0 is never enumerated, even when its order passes the small test.

Everything stays exact. `python3 -m pytest -q tests` passes (280 passed, 19 skipped). Every
benchmark answer below was checked against nauty, and no answer was wrong.

## 1. The reduction

Take connected X and Y and an oriented edge f = (u0, u1) of X. L_r is the set of vertices at
distance r from {u0, u1}, and X_r is the induced subgraph on L_0 ∪ … ∪ L_r, with colors. Distances
to f are the same in X_r as in X, so automorphisms of X_{r+1} that fix u0 and u1 preserve the
layers and restrict to automorphisms of X_r. The code keeps

    G_r = Aut_f(X_r),    Iso_{f→f'}(X_r, Y_r) = G_r σ_r,

starting from the trivial group on (u0, u1). Each step r → r+1 is two string problems:

1. **Up-sets.** For w ∈ L_{r+1}, up(w) = N(w) ∩ L_r. Vertices with the same up-set S form a twin
   class T_S, and |T_S| ≤ d − 1, because every v ∈ L_r already has a neighbour in L_{r−1} (for
   r = 0, the other end of f). The positions are the G_r-orbits of the up-sets that occur in X.
   The string is the sorted colors of T_S, empty when no vertex has up-set S. The target's
   up-sets are pulled back by σ_r. If one falls outside the positions the step is refuted, since x
   is 0 on that whole orbit. `Solver.solve` runs on the action of G_r on these positions (an
   `ActionHom`) and gives G'' σ''.
2. **Twin classes and inner edges.** H = ρ(G'') ⋉ ∏ Sym(T_S ∩ color class) acts on L_{r+1}. Here ρ
   is the action of G'' on the up-sets, and the k-th member of T_S goes to the k-th member of
   T_{S^g}. When L_{r+1} has inner edges, the string lives on the H-orbits of X's inner edges:
   1 on an edge, 0 off one. The target's string is the adjacency of ψ(·), where ψ matches twin
   classes by rank. `Solver.solve` gives Aut_H(x) h0. With no inner edges, Aut_H(x) = H.

**Lifting (why it is exact).** Each h ∈ Aut_H(x) permutes the up-sets as ρ(g) for some g ∈ G''.
The map g⁺ = (g on X_r, h on L_{r+1}) sends T_S into T_{S^g}. So it keeps the L_r–L_{r+1} edges
and the colors, and it keeps the inner edges because h preserves x. Hence g⁺ ∈ G_{r+1}.
Conversely, every element of G_{r+1} restricts to G'' and to some element of Aut_H(x). Those
fixing L_{r+1} pointwise are exactly ker ρ. So G_{r+1} = ⟨g⁺ : h a generator of Aut_H(x)⟩ ·
ker ρ. Its composition factors come from G'' and from Sym(|T_S|) with |T_S| ≤ d − 1, so by
induction every group handed to the solver lies in Γ̂_{d−1}. The new coset representative is
σ_{r+1} = (g0 then σ'') on X_r and ψ∘h0 on L_{r+1}. This is Luks's step. Helfgott (arXiv:1710.04574,
Exercise B.3 (c)) takes all ≤ d-subsets of L_r as positions; the code uses only the orbits of
the ones that occur.

**Whole graphs.**
* **Invariant filter.** Oriented edges get an invariant: the end colors plus the multiset of
  (distance to u0, distance to u1, color). The invariant multisets of X and Y are compared first.
* **Aut(X).** It is generated by Aut_{f0}(X) plus one isomorphism (X, f0) → (X, f) for each tested
  f in the invariant class of f0. Edges already in the current orbit of f0 are skipped. So is the
  orbit of any edge that failed, since a failure is Aut(X)-invariant.
* **The isomorphism.** Fix one f'0 in Y. The code runs one test (X, f) → (Y, f'0) per
  Aut(X)-orbit in the matching invariant class.
* **Memo.** Tests from the same root reuse its groups, so the solver's memo answers them in
  one-mode.
* **Components.** Components are grouped into isomorphism classes. Aut(X) is then generated by
  each class representative's Aut(c) plus the swaps of isomorphic components.
* **Colors.** Vertex colors are supported. With `refine=True`, joint 1-WL runs first.
* **Root choice.** `root="class"` (the default) roots at the smallest invariant class.
  `root="degree"` roots at an edge of maximum degree sum.

**First version.** The first implementation (commit 4beea45) used a down-edge wreath product
τ(G') ⋉ ∏_v Sym(δ(v)) over the edges from L_r to L_{r+1}, with a pair string. It is also exact.
But it gave every vertex a Sym(δ(v)) factor that the pair string had to break, one orbit at a
time. The twin-class form is much faster:

| instance | down-edge form | twin-class form |
|---|---|---|
| 3reg400 | 6.7 s | 0.86 s |
| Q7 | 18.8 s | 0.11 s |
| CFI(3reg30) | 1.4 s | 0.50 s |

## 2. (M) and instrumentation

`Solver(cameron_no_enum=True, cameron_q0=4)`:

* **Small groups.** A small group in `_cont`, or a small primitive quotient in the dispatcher, is
  enumerated only if its order is not divisible by |Alt(q0+1)| = 60, or if recognition finds no
  Cameron structure with m > q0.
* **Cameron path.** Johnson actions and abstract giants with m > q0, and product actions of order
  ≥ 60^r, take the giant step or product refinement.
* **Natural giants.** These were never enumerated.
* **Solvable groups.** Every group met when d ≤ 5 is solvable, so its order is not divisible by
  5 and nothing changes.

Stats:

| stat | meaning |
|---|---|
| `cameron_skip_enum` | small groups not enumerated in `_cont` |
| `cameron_small_<kind>` | small quotients sent down a Cameron path |
| `enum_quotient`, `enum_quotient_max_order`, `enum_quotient_max_degree` | enumerated primitive quotients |
| `alt_section_max` | the largest m of an Alt(m) section met; a lower bound on the composition width |
| `luks_twin_max` | the largest twin class, which bounds the Sym factors added (≤ d − 1, checked in tests) |
| `luks_group_max_log2_order` | log2 of the largest G_r |
| `luks_phase{1,2}_max_degree`, `luks_phase2_max_log2_order` | the sizes of the groups handed to the solver |

## 3. Measurements

`bench/bounded.py` produces these; the data is in `bench/results/bounded-r4-*.json`.

**Pipelines compared.**
* new: `bounded_iso`, `Solver(cameron_no_enum=True, certificates=False)`.
* default: `graph_iso`, `Solver(certificates=False)`.
* nauty: pynauty 2.8.8.1, dense nauty with default options. The time covers three calls: the
  certificates of X and Y, then autgrp. Python graph construction is ≤ 0.03 s.

**Conditions.** Seconds per instance, one process, `nice`. The machine load varied between 3 and
14 (another user's jobs), so single timings are noisy by about ±30%. "twist" and "-non" are the

**Checking.** Every answer was checked: the decision against nauty, σ, every generator, and
|⟨gens⟩| (sympy) against nauty's |Aut|.

### Johnson gadgets (`jgad`, degree 7; built to exercise (M))

Each hub has 5 children and 10 grandchildren on the 2-sets of the children, and hubs are joined

### Path counters on the natural families

The natural families are CFI, random regular graphs, hypercubes and multipedes. On all of them
`luks_twin_max` ≤ d − 1, as the reduction guarantees, and the largest enumerated primitive
quotient has order 2, 6, 24 or 72. `alt_section_max` was 0 everywhere except 6reg150, where it
was 5. There one small Johnson-action quotient (one group, met twice) took the Johnson path under
(M) instead of being enumerated, and three small natural Sym(5) quotients took the giant step in
both modes. So (M) changed nothing measurable on the natural families. With d ≤ 5 the groups are
solvable, so (M) cannot fire at all.

With d ≥ 6, the Sym(5) twin factors do appear (luks_twin_max = 5–7), but they almost never reach
the dispatcher as a small non-natural action. Either the string leaves them invariant, as on
hypercubes, where log2|G_r| ≤ 12, or they are split by refinement or reach it as natural giants.
(M) acts only on the constructed jgad family, and only with the root on a hub edge. There the
largest enumerated quotient drops from order 120 to 6, and the speed is about the same. The giant
steps branch (3022 branches on jgad24) where the default dispatcher enumerated.

The down-edge first version did meet Alt(5) and Alt(6) sections on hypercubes. There (M) cut Q6
from 2.9 s to 1.2 s and Q7 from 42 s to 19 s. The gap is a constant factor here, because
`enum_limit` is the constant 500: under (M) only quotients of order ≤ 500 change path. HJ's
small test grows with n, and that is where (M) matters asymptotically.

## 4. Scaling, and consistency with the claimed bound

Least-squares fits of log t against log n (`bench/bounded.py fit`) give exponents per pipeline.

| family | n range | new | default | nauty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CFI(3-reg) | 60–800 | 2.2 | 2.3 | 3.4 |
| CFI(3-reg) | 200–800 | 2.7 | 2.5–3.0 | 3.5 |
| CFI(4-reg) | 80–240 | 2.1–2.4 | 2.4–2.7 | 2.6 |
| 3-reg | 100–600 | 2.1 | 2.0 | 2.9 |
| 4-reg | 100–400 | 2.8–3.1 | 1.8–2.1 | 2.8 |
| 6-reg | 60–200 | 2.3–2.7 | 2.0 | 2.6 |
| Q_d | 64–256 | 1.9 | 2.2 | 1.8 |
| multipede | 88–440 | 2.7–2.9 | 2.8–3.1 | 2.6 |

Fitting t = exp(c · log n · log log n) instead gives c ≈ 0.5–1.45 for the new pipeline across the
families.

* **Fixed d says nothing about the bound.** For fixed d the claimed bound exp(O(log N (log d +
  log log N))) is n^{O(log log n)}. Any polynomial is consistent with it, and so is Luks's own
  n^{O(d)}. Over n ≤ 800, log log n only moves from about 1.4 to 1.9, so a polynomial cannot be
  told apart from n^{c log log n}. These sizes cannot confirm or refute the asymptotics. All the
  data shows is that nothing grows faster than a low polynomial on these families.
* **Hypercubes are the only family with growing d** (d = log2 n). There the claim is
  n^{O(log log n)}, and the measurement shows about n^{1.9}. That is uninformative too: the
  strings never break the Sym(d − 1) symmetry, so the groups stay tiny (|G_r| ≤ 2^12).
* **The groups are small in practice.** On the CFI families log2|G_r| grows linearly in the base
  size, up to 79 for CFI(3reg80) and 64 for CFI(4reg12). That is more than |Aut| = 2^(b/2+1),
  because X_r has not yet closed the base's cycles. On the other natural families log2|G_r| stays
  ≤ 30. Phase-2 groups reach 2^68. The cost is dominated by pure-Python Schreier–Sims inside the
  solver's orbit chain and by small enumerations, not by branching: the natural families branch
  at most 9 times. The one-mode iso runs cost about as much as the first runs.
* **Against the other pipelines.**
  * **CFI, hypercubes and multipedes:** the new pipeline beats the default one, by 5–40× on CFI
    for n ≥ 200, 15× on Q8, and 2.5–5× on multipedes.
  * **Random regular graphs:** it loses, by 1–11×. There the default pipeline's 2-WL front end
    and individualization finish quickly.
  * **nauty:** it is faster than both pipelines everywhere, by 50–140× on cfi4, cubes and
    multipedes. The gap is smallest where this dense nauty without invariants grows like n^3: on
    CFI(3-reg) the new pipeline is 1.7–3.8× slower for n = 300–800, and on random 3-regular graphs
    1.3–1.4× slower at n = 600.
