## 1. Is W the right sharpening?

### 1.1 Faithfulness

W's form ($k$ absolute; hatch $\lvert\pi_R(C)\rvert\le f(b)b^{cM}$; $g(b)$ arbitrary extra walks; $s(b)$ exceptional coordinates per walk) is **at least as generous as** w27. w27 §2.5 allowed "$f(b)$ walks on $f(b)$ coordinates" with $O(1)$ values elsewhere. W allows arbitrarily wide extra walks, and per-walk exceptions on top. So refuting W refutes w27's lead.

* **Hatch.** A *per-coordinate* notion ("$\lvert\pi_j(C)\rvert\le f(b)$") is vacuous, since $\lvert\pi_j(C)\rvert\le b!$ always. The joint size $\lvert\pi_R(C)\rvert$ against an $N^{O(1)}$ budget is the only non-vacuous reading of "coordinates where $C$ is enumerable". It also matches w27's own heuristic: "a regular part has $\le N$ elements".
* **w27's hypothesis "large point stabilisers"** holds for the family: $C_v=S_n^M$, of order $b^{\Theta(M\log\log b)}$.
* **Robustness.** If the hatch $R_W$ may depend on the walk, and the number of extra walks is unbounded, the refutation still holds. Each walk with $r\ne0$ is wide on $\ge d$ coordinates, and $\lvert H\rvert^{d}$ exceeds the budget.

So W is not a strawman.

### 1.2 How thin the refutation is (what survives)

1. **The margin is $\log\log b$.** The hatch with $R=[M]$ is allowed as soon as $c\ge\log_b\lvert H\rvert=1+\log_2(n!)/(n-1)$: 2.73, 3.05, 3.31, 3.88, 4.76 for $n=5,7,9,15,31$. This is $\sim\log_2\log_2b$. So **W with $c(b)\ge\log_b\lvert H\rvert$ is not refuted by these instances.** For primitive $H$ with odd girth $>k$ I found nothing with $\log_b\lvert H\rvert\gg\log\log b$. Kneser $K(m,t)$ with odd girth $>k$ forces $m\le2t(1+O(1/k))$, which gives $\Theta(\log m)$ again. For imprimitive $H$ see the next item.
2. **Imprimitive blow-ups are a strawman refutation, and the memo is right to set them aside.** $H=S_s\wr D_L$ on $C_L[\overline K_s]$ has odd girth $L$ and $\log_b\lvert H\rvert\approx b$. With Cor B.1's argument it refutes W for every $c(b)=o(b)$ and absolute $k<L$. But the obstruction lives on the quotient $D_L^M$ acting on $[L]^M$, which has $(2L)^M\le N^{O(1)}$ elements. W's coordinate-subset hatch cannot see an enumerable *quotient*. Any repaired W must allow hatches on sections, not only on coordinate subsets.
3. **Only the "all of $\pi_1$" form fails.** By Theorem B′, $\pi_1$ *is* normally generated by 4-narrow walks plus $\kappa\le M$ further walks. So **W with $g=g(b,M)\ge M$ holds on the family.** Its quotient $\pi_1/K^4\cong\mathcal D$ has trivial $C$-action. This is what W\* encodes. For abelian gains it is algorithmically sufficient (Cor E.1).
4. **$k$ of order $\log b$:** Theorem B′ gives narrow generation at $k=n=\log_2b+1$. With $k=\Theta(\log b)$, box enumeration costs $N^{\Theta(\log b)}$, the same as brute force over $(b!)^M$.

So the honest conclusion is that **narrow normal generation of all of $\pi_1$ with absolute $k$ and absolute $c$ is false.** The forms that survive the family are:

* W with $c(b)\gtrsim\log\log b$;
* W with $\ge\kappa$ extra walks;
* W\*.

### 1.3 Strengthening of Cor B.1 (proved here)

**Prop B.1′.** Let $k$ be fixed, $n>k$ odd, $b=2^{n-1}$, and $\ell:=\log_b\lvert H\rvert$. Then W$(k,c,s,g,f)$ fails for every $c<\ell$ and every $s,g,f$.

*Proof.* Take $F=\{B_1,\dots,B_K\}$, a partition of $[M]$ into $K$ blocks of size $M/K$. So $\mathcal D$ is the direct sum of $K$ repetition codes and $\kappa=K$. An allowed $R$ has $\lvert R\rvert\le(c/\ell)M+O_b(1)$.

* A walk with $r\ne0$ is wide (has $\ge n$ values) on every coordinate of the blocks in $\mathrm{supp}\,r$.
* It is admissible only if each such block is *nearly covered*: $\lvert B_i\setminus R\rvert\le s(b)$.
* At most $\lvert R\rvert/(M/K-s(b))=(c/\ell)K(1+o(1))$ blocks are nearly covered.
* So the $r$-images of admissible walks span at most $(c/\ell)K(1+o(1))$ dimensions, plus $g(b)$ from the extra walks.

Choose $K>(g(b)+2)/(1-c/\ell)$, then $M\to\infty$. ∎

So the memo's GV choice ($\delta\approx0.11$) loses a factor of about 9. For $c=1$ and $k\le4$, already $n=5$ ($b=16$, $\ell=2.73$) refutes W. The memo's "$\log_2n$ large relative to $c$" is unnecessary. The threshold for this family is exactly $c=\ell$.

## 2. Theorem B

### 2.1 The homomorphism

$r_j(W)$ counts the steps that move coordinate $j$, mod 2.

* **Well defined on homotopy classes.** A backtrack changes each count by 2.
* **A homomorphism.** It is additive. Equivalently, it is the functional $z\mapsto\lvert z\cap E_j\rvert$ on $H_1(\Gamma;F_2)$.
* **Conjugation-invariant.** The target is abelian, so lasso tails contribute $2\times$.
* **$C$-trivial.** Any base-group element preserves the set of coordinates moved by each edge. This holds for every $C\le S_b^M$, not only $H^M$.
* **Onto.** Pattern $I$ repeated $L$ times, with $L$ odd and each $j\in I$ running an odd closed walk of $\Gamma_1$ of length exactly $L$. This exists at every vertex once $\Gamma_1$ is connected and non-bipartite; vertex-transitivity is not needed. The argument works in every component, so disconnected $\Gamma_F$ (when $\bigcup F\ne[M]$) causes no basepoint issue.

### 2.2 The lower bound

If $r_j(W)=1$, the $j$-projection with stationary steps deleted is an odd closed walk in $\Gamma_1$. It contains an odd cycle, which has $\ge g$ distinct vertices. These are $\ge g$ distinct **values** at $j$, exactly as $\mathrm{val}_j$ is defined.

* Conjugates cannot help: $r(pWp^{-1})=r(W)$.
* Generators in $\ker r$ contribute nothing, and the theorem only counts those with $r\ne0$.
* Their images span $\mathcal D$, so at least $\kappa$ of them have $r\ne0$, each of weight $\ge d$.

**Edge cases.**

* $d=1$ (e.g. Cartesian $F$): true but empty; a single exceptional coordinate absorbs it.
* Dependent patterns: $\kappa=\mathrm{rank}$, still correct.
* Every $\le4$-walk in the $n=5$, $M=2$ graphs has $r=0$ (checked).

### 2.3 Parameters

* **Folded cube.** $b=2^{n-1}$; odd girth $n$ (odd walks lift to $x\to\bar x$ in $Q_n$); $H=2^{n-1}{:}S_n$ is the full automorphism group (Clebsch graph at $n=5$: order 1920, rebuilt); primitive for odd $n$ ($F_2^n/\langle1\rangle\cong$ the even-weight module, irreducible for odd $n$).
* **Odd graphs.** $b=\binom{2t+1}{t}$, $g=2t+1\sim\log_2b$, $H=S_{2t+1}$.
* **Both.** $\lvert H\rvert=b^{\Theta(\log\log b)}$; $g$ checked by BFS for $n=5$ and $t=2,3$.
* **Adversarial $R$.** $\lvert R\rvert$ is bounded unconditionally because $\pi_R(C)=H^R$. An admissible generator has $\mathrm{supp}\,r\subseteq R\cup S_W$, so it has $r=0$ whenever $\lvert R\rvert+s(b)<d$. That is the whole proof; §1.3 sharpens the count.

### 2.4 Remark 1 (double cover, HT2 setting): factor 2

In $C\le S_b^M\times S_b^M$, coordinate $j$ of $V$ and coordinate $j$ of $V'$ are **different** coordinates, and a walk alternates sides. The $\ge g$ values of an odd cycle are split between the two.

**Explicit walk** (`r5_misc.py dc`). Folded 5-cube, Cartesian $F$. Run the 5-cycle on coordinate 0, then on coordinate 1: length 10, $r=(1,1)\in\mathcal D_0$, and **3 values on each of the four side-coordinates**, while $g=5$.

So "Theorem B applies with $\kappa-1$, $d$" must read: $\ge\lceil g/2\rceil$ values on at least one of $(j,V),(j,V')$ for each $j\in\mathrm{supp}\,r$. The refutation of the HT2 form needs $n>2k$. Harmless asymptotically.

### 2.5 Remark 2 (odd $p$): no coupling

With undirected generators $\pm e_i$, the coordinates of one pattern move with independent signs. Take $p=3$, $n=4$, pattern $\{0,1\}$, length 4:

* coordinate 0: $+e_1,+e_2,+e_3,+e_4$, so $\lambda=1$;
* coordinate 1: $+e_1,-e_1,+e_1,-e_1$, so $\lambda=0$.

The obstruction vector $(1,0)$ has weight 1. So the $Z_p$ analogue gives only single-coordinate obstructions ($d=1$), like the folded Johnson case. "By the same argument" is wrong as to the coupling, which is the point of Theorem B. A $Z_p$ coupling needs directed patterns, i.e. a non-symmetric $\Gamma$ or a $C$ with diagonal signs.

## 4. Theorem A

### 4.1 Re-derivation and improvement to $k=3$

I re-derived the memo's proof; every step is right:

* boundary edges via the 2-point stabiliser;
* rungs via $\mathrm{Stab}(c)$ transitive on the rest;
* adjacency of $w_i$ to $v_i'$ via an element fixing $e$;
* the ladder decomposition and the value counts.

It works for disconnected $\Gamma$, since everything is local to the component. The needed edges always exist.

**Key observation.** For $C=\prod N_j$ with 2-transitive $N_j$, the $C$-orbit of a pair $(u,v)$ is determined by $D(u,v)=\{j:u_j\ne v_j\}$. So **every invariant graph is $\Gamma_F(K_b)$ for some $F$**. Theorem A is about NEPS of complete graphs (§8), and the local group plays no further role.

**Theorem A$_3$ [PROVEN here].** Assume $b\ge3$ and the same hypotheses. Then $\pi_1$ is normally generated by closed walks with $\le3$ values per coordinate. Three is optimal: for $M=1$, $\Gamma=K_b$, the 2-narrow walks are trivial.

*Proof.* Fix $j$ moved by $W$, and $I_0\in F$ with $j\in I_0$, the difference set of some step of $W$.

1. **Subdivide.** Take a step $v\to u$ with $v_j,u_j\ne0$ distinct and $I=D(v,u)$. Let $y$ have $y_j=0$, $y_l\in\{0,1,2\}\setminus\{v_l,u_l\}$ for $l\in I\setminus j$, and $y_l=v_l$ otherwise. Then $v,u,y$ is a triangle with $\le3$ values per coordinate. Replace $v\to u$ by $v\to y\to u$. Afterwards every change of the $j$-value passes through 0.
2. **Recolour.** Each maximal run of value $c\notin\{0,1\}$ (positions $s..t$) now lies between 0-runs. Set $v_i'=v_i[j\mapsto1]$. Difference sets are unchanged because $0,c,1$ are distinct, so the walk with the run recoloured is a walk.
   * *Rungs.* Any $w$ with $D(w,v_i)=I_0$ and $w_j=0$ is adjacent to both $v_i$ and $v_i'$.
   * *Rung change at $i$.* The 4-walk $v_i',w,v_i,w',v_i'$ has values $\{0,1,c\}$ at $j$ and $\{v_{i,l},w_l,w'_l\}$ elsewhere.
   * *Hexagon from $i$ to $i+1$.* Choose $w_i,w_{i+1}$ by: $w_{i,l}=v_{i+1,l}$ and $w_{i+1,l}=v_{i,l}$ when $l\in I_0\setminus j$ and $v_{i,l}\ne v_{i+1,l}$; a common third value when they are equal; $w_l=v_l$ off $I_0$. This gives $\le2$ values off $j$ and 3 at $j$.
   * *End rungs* are $v_{s-1}$ and $v_{t+1}$ themselves (backtracks).
3. **Finish.** The $j$-values are now in $\{0,1\}$. Later subdivisions use values in $\{0,1,2\}$, so the final walk is 3-narrow. ∎

**Checks.**

* `r2_cert.py` runs this on 3000 random closed walks ($3\le b\le9$, $M\le6$, random $F$): 989,351 moves. Each move is a subpath replacement whose tile is a closed walk of the graph with $\le3$ values per coordinate, or a backtrack insertion or deletion.
* `r1_thmA.py`, homology, **exhaustive over all $F$** up to coordinate permutation: least $k=3$ in all 357 (family, $p$) cases. That is 5 families at $M=2$ and 39 at $M=3$, for $b\in\{3,4,5\}$ ($b=5$, $M=3$ over $F_2,F_3$ only), with $p\in\{2,3,5\}$.
* $\pi_1$ level ($b=4,5$, $M=2$, all 5 families): the 3-box relators eliminate every generator, so the quotient is trivial.

**E1, by contrast.** E1's "42/42 (graph, prime) pairs" are about 9 distinct graphs. Different 2-transitive $H$ on the same $b$ give the same $\Gamma_F$, and several random draws coincide (identical lines in `RESULTS-e1.txt`). "Graph-prime" means (graph, prime $p$); it is not a hypothesis. The 3-versus-4 discrepancy was slack, not a mismatch.

### 4.2 Theorem A′: the constant 4 is sharp

Take $M=2$. Coordinate 2 uses the values $\{0,1\}$, and $C=S_b\times(S_2\times S_{b-2})$. The graph is $(x,\varepsilon)\sim(y,1-\varepsilon)$ for $x\ne y$; on the non-trivial component it is $K_b\times K_2$. Then $N_1=S_b$, $J=\{1\}$, and A′'s hypothesis holds.

**Claim.** Walks with $\le3$ values at coordinate 1 do not normally generate $\pi_1$, for any $b\ge4$.

*Proof.* A 3-value box induces $K_3\times K_2=C_6$. Over $F_2$ its hexagon is $s(ab)+s(bc)+s(ca)$, with $s(xy):=(x,y)+(y,x)$ in the edge space, where edge $(x,y)$ means $(x,0)(y,1)$. So the span is $s(Z(K_b))$, of dimension $\binom{b-1}2$. A 4-cycle with edges $ab,cb,cd,ad$ is not symmetric, so it is not in the span. The codimension is $(b^2-3b)/2$. ∎

`r3_aprime.py` confirms rank $\binom{b-1}2$ for $4\le b\le10$ over $F_2$, $F_3$, $F_5$ and $Z$. So A$_3$ does not extend to A′: Theorem A needs every coordinate to have $b_j\ge3$ and a 2-transitive kernel.

### 4.3 "Exactly when" (§7)

"Walk-based narrow generation holds exactly when coordinate kernels are 2-transitive" is false in the "only if" direction. Take $H=S_6$ on the 15 pairs (rank 3, not 2-transitive):

* both orbital graphs, $T(6)$ and $K(6,2)$, have $\pi_1$ trivial modulo triangles and 4-cycles (Tietze, $M=1$);
* the same holds at homology level for the $M=2$ Cartesian graphs, over $F_2$ and $F_3$.

**Fix:** "2-transitive kernels suffice (Theorem A′); the obstruction is odd girth or deck data of the local graphs (Theorem B)."

## 5. Theorem B′, O1, O2, Prop E, Cor E.1, the gap

### 5.1 Theorem B′

Re-derived step by step:

* the Cayley presentation (needs $\bigcup F=[M]$ for connectedness, or else work in a component; $S_F$ generates $T^{\bigcup F}$ for odd $n$);
* $\pi_1/\langle\langle\text{commutators}\rangle\rangle\cong\mathcal C$;
* $r_j=\varepsilon\circ\mu_j$;
* $\ker(\text{marginal})=R_I$ by the dimension count $1+\lvert I\rvert(n-1)$;
* the cross elements span $\{(\delta_{I,j}):\sum_{I\ni j}\delta_{I,j}=0\}$.

All correct.

**Reproduced.**

* Coranks of the $\le4$-walk span ($F_2$ / $F_3$): cartesian 2/0, tensor 1/0, mix 2/0, dependent 2/0, equal to $\mathrm{rk}\,\mathcal D$ / 0.
* Integer quotient $H_1/B=Z/2$ for $n=5,7$ at $M=1$.
* $\pi_1$ level: $\pi_1/\langle\langle\le4\text{-walks}\rangle\rangle=\langle x\mid x^2\rangle$ ($n=5,7$, $M=1$) and $\langle x,y\mid x^2,y^2,[x,y]\rangle$ ($n=5$, $M=2$ Cartesian).

**Extension.** For dependent $F$, each relation $\sum_{I\in F'}1_I=0$ gives a closed walk using a fixed direction at every coordinate. It has $\le2$ values per coordinate and length $\lvert F'\rvert$. So $\ker r=K^k$ for $4\le k<n$ holds for **every** $F$; only "length $\le4$" needs independence. Code-level check (pentagon $F$, $n=5$, `r5_misc.py dep`): $\dim\mathcal C_0=101$, rectangles and crosses give 100, and one relation walk makes 101.

### 5.2 O1, O2

* **O1** is right. Odd projections use every direction. Pointwise stabilisers are trivial for $n\ge3$ ($\bar e_i$ distinct). The orbit bound is $\lvert H\rvert^{d}/(2L)$.
* **O2: $\lvert\mathrm{Stab}_T(z)\rvert\le2$** has a one-line proof. A translation fixing an odd $z$ must fix an edge of $z$, so it is some $\bar e_i$. For $n\ge5$, two distinct $\bar e_i$ do not generate a group of order $\le2$.
* **Exact $m_H$ for $n=5$** (`r5_misc.py mH`): every $z$ with non-trivial stabiliser is fixed by a prime-order element. Enumerating $\mathrm{Fix}(h)\cap Z$ for representatives gives 8464 odd vectors. The maximum $\lvert\mathrm{Stab}\rvert$ is 10 and $m_H=192$, as claimed. The $n=7,9$ "structured tests" and $\lvert E\rvert\le2^{O(n)}$ remain speculation; I did not test them.

### 5.3 Prop E

The proof is correct:

* $\delta(ts)=\delta(t)\circ s+\delta(s)=\delta(t)+\delta(s)$ because $s$ is trivial on $Q$;
* $S=C't_0$;
* the solutions are $\ker\delta\cdot t_0'$.

Add the hypothesis that $C$ preserves $X$ (or use its setwise stabiliser).

Random gains make the test vacuous ($C'=1$). So `propE2` solves for gains $x\in F_2^E$ whose $B$-values are $T$-invariant. Over 30 instances on the folded 5-cube with $C=H$ (1920 elements, brute force): $\lvert C'\rvert\in\{32,\dots,384\}$, $\delta\ne0$ every time, and in all 20 solvable instances the solution set equals the predicted coset of $\ker\delta$. One variant adds the parity character, so $y\ne x^t$ on $Q$.

### 5.4 Cor E.1

* **S-W `theo:obj`** (read in `r4-sources/w1s-arxiv-1806.07466/x/articles/hereditarilyFiniteObjects.tex` L19–27): time $2^{O(k)}n^{O(1)}$, with $k$ the largest $\Delta$-orbit on the ground set and no other hypothesis.
* **Applied here.** Take $\Delta=H^M$ on $U=[M]\times[b]$ (orbits of size $b$). Encode a walk as a tuple of vertices, and a vertex as a tuple of points $(j,v_j)$. This encoding is injective and equivariant (STR Prop 4.1). The object size is $\mathrm{poly}(N)$, because $\deg\Gamma_F=\sum_I n^{\lvert I\rvert}\le MN^{o(1)}$. Then $\delta$ is computed from generators on the $\kappa$ length-$n$ walks, and $\ker\delta$ via a degree-$2\kappa\,\mathrm{rk}A[2]$ permutation representation. **FPT holds as claimed, mod S-W.**
* **Scope (low–medium).** The §0 sentence ("it is FPT there") and table row E.1 ("the Theorem-B family") overclaim. Cor E.1 needs Theorem B′, which is proved only for folded cubes. For a general $\Gamma_1$ in Theorem B, $\pi_1/K^k$ can be large with non-trivial $C$-action, and Prop E does not apply.
  * Odd graphs at $M=1$: the needed fact is Matsushita's Prop 5.3 ($\pi_1$ modulo 4- and 6-cycles is $Z/2$, §8).
  * My numerics: $H_1/B=Z/2$ for $t=3,4$; for $O_4$ at $M=2$ (Cartesian and tensor), the corank equals $\mathrm{rk}\,\mathcal D$ over $F_2$ and is 0 over $F_3$.
  * So the extension to odd graphs is plausible but unproved.
  * **Fix:** say "folded-cube instances".
* **HT2 gains are $S_b$-valued.** For holonomy in an abelian $A\le S_b$, $S_b$-switching is conjugacy by one $g\in S_b$ per component. Enumerating $g$ costs $b!$. Also, the double-cover version has $\pi_1^{\rm ev}/K\cong\mathcal D_0$. Both fit in $f(b)$; the memo should say so.

### 5.5 The gap

Sound. An explicit witness inside Theorem A's setting ($M=1$, $b=4$, $C=S_4$, $\Gamma=K_4$, gains in $S_3\le S_4$): $x=(\tau,\tau,\tau)$ and $y=(\tau,\tau,\sigma)$ on the triangles at a star, with $\tau=(12)$ and $\sigma=(01)$.

* All four triangle holonomies are transpositions. Every 3-narrow closed walk lies in a triangle, so every 3-narrow walk has conjugate holonomy under $x$ and $y$.
* The holonomy groups are $Z_2$ and $S_3$, so no $t\in S_4$ makes them switching-equivalent.
* There are 792 such pairs in total.

## 6. "Survives Theorems G, G+, C and Prop N"

I checked this against w27 as corrected (G and G+ are XP in $\rho$; C needs $R$ given and Pálfy–Wolf).

* **Height-2 shape.** Bijection blocks on the edges of the double cover $\Gamma_F^{\rm dc}\subseteq V\times V'$, constant blocks elsewhere. The surviving top contains $\mathrm{diag}(H^M)$. Folded cubes and odd graphs are stable (their bipartite double covers, $Q_n$ and the bipartite Kneser graph, have side-preserving automorphism group exactly $H$). I did not compute $\mathrm{Aut}_{T_1\times T_2}$ for mixed $F$; a larger top only removes routes.
* **G, G+.** The degree is $N^{o(1)}$, so pinning sets have $\rho\ge N^{1-o(1)}$. Bijection blocks give the same histogram to all columns of a block, so G+ separates nothing.
* **C.** $H$ is non-soluble for $n\ge5$ and $t\ge2$, so $r=M$ and the cost is $(b!)^{M+2}$.
* **Prop N** is irrelevant.

All correct.

**Framing gap (medium).** "$H$ non-enumerable" is wrong: $\lvert H\rvert\le b!$ is tiny. What is non-enumerable is $C$, and only barely: $\lvert C\rvert=\lvert H\rvert^M=N^{\Theta(\log\log b)}$. Enumerate $t\in C$ and test $S_b$-switching equivalence for each ($b!\cdot\mathrm{poly}$ per component). This solves **any** gain problem on the family, non-abelian included, in $f(b)N^{O(\log\log b)}$. Reduction 0 with "enumerable" read as $N^{O(\log\log b)}$ already does it.

So "non-abelian gains on this family are open" means only "can a $\log\log b$ exponent be removed". The family is a test for FPT-versus-XP with a near-constant exponent, not a hardness candidate in any stronger sense. The memo's own conclusion ("not yet a hard candidate") stands, a fortiori.

## 8. Prior art

Searches on 2026-11-09 were thin. Sources:

* T. Matsushita, *Fundamental groups of neighborhood complexes*, J. Math. Sci. Univ. Tokyo 24 (2017) 321–353 ([abstract](https://www.ms.u-tokyo.ac.jp/journal/abstract/jms240302.html); read locally in `r4-sources/w53-matsushita/`).

* Chalopin–Chepoi–Hirai–Osajda, *Weakly modular graphs and nonpositive curvature* ([arXiv:1409.3892](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.3892)).

What each covers:

* **Theorem B's mechanism is known.** Matsushita's $r$-fundamental group $\pi_1^r(G)$ is $\pi_1$ of the 2-complex with 2-cells on all $2s$-cycles, $s\le r$. It comes with the length-parity homomorphism $\pi_1^r\to Z/2$. Prop 5.3 there shows $\pi_1^3(KG_{2k+1,k})\cong Z/2$; this is exactly the $M=1$ odd-graph case of the B′ statement. He uses it as an obstruction (no homomorphism $KG_{2k+1,k}\to C_5$) that odd girth alone does not give. The folded-cube $M=1$ case is the antipodal quotient of the simply connected square complex of $Q_n$.
* **The pattern graph $\Gamma_F$ is the NEPS** (non-complete extended $p$-sum) of Cvetković with basis $\{1_I\}$. Connectivity and component counts of NEPS (Stevanović 1999) are governed by bipartite factors and the basis. $r$ is the deck map of the NEPS of the bipartite double covers. I did not see Stevanović's full text, so I cannot say whether the $F_2$-rank formulation is stated there.
* **Theorem A, Cartesian case.** Hamming graphs are quasi-median, hence weakly modular, and their triangle-square complexes are simply connected (CCHO). That gives $k=3$ for Cartesian $F$. I found nothing for general $F$, i.e. NEPS of complete graphs.

**What is new.** The coupling of parity across coordinates by a code with large distance, used as a lower bound on narrow normal generation, and the resulting refutation of W. Theorem A for general $F$ (and $k=3$, §4.1). The $C$-trivial-quotient reduction (Prop E). All elementary.

## 9. Statements I would sign

* **(A)** Let $C=\prod_jN_j$ with each $N_j\le S_b$ 2-transitive and $b\ge3$. Then every $C$-invariant graph is $\Gamma_F(K_b)$, and each component's $\pi_1$ is normally generated by closed walks with $\le3$ values per coordinate. This is sharp. Theorem A′ holds with 4, and 4 is sharp.
* **(B)** Theorem B as stated. W$(k,c,s,g,f)$ fails for all $s,g,f$ whenever $k<n$ and $c<\log_b\lvert 2^{n-1}{:}S_n\rvert$, with $b=2^{n-1}$ and $n$ odd. Hence it fails for all absolute $k,c$. In the HT2 (double-cover) form, replace $g$ by $\lceil g/2\rceil$.
* **(C)** Theorem B′ for all $F$, with "length $\le4$" for independent $F$. Props O1 and O2, with $m_H=192$ at $n=5$.
* **(D)** Prop E, with $C$ preserving $X$. Cor E.1 for folded-cube instances, mod S-W `theo:obj`.
* **(E)** On the folded-cube family every gain problem is in time $f(b)N^{O(\log\log b)}$. The abelian one is in time $2^{O(b)}N^{O(1)}$ (mod S-W).
* **(F)** The normal-versus-generating gap, with the $K_4$ witness.

I do not certify: the novelty claims; the odd-graph extension of B′ and E.1 beyond $M=1$ (Matsushita) and my $O_4$, $M=2$ numerics; W\*; anything about non-abelian gains.
