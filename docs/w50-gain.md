## 1. Sharpened statement

**Setting.** $V=[b]^M$, $N:=b^M$. $C\le S_b^M$ (base group only: no coordinate permutations). $\Gamma$ a $C$-invariant graph on $V$. A *closed walk* $W=(v_0,\dots,v_L=v_0)$ in $\Gamma$. For $j\in[M]$, $\mathrm{val}_j(W):=\lvert\{v_i(j)\}\rvert$. $W$ is **$k$-narrow on $J\subseteq[M]$** if $\mathrm{val}_j(W)\le k$ for $j\in J$. Equivalently, $W$ lies in a box $\prod_jB_j$ with $\lvert B_j\rvert\le k$ on $J$. Write $K^k_J\trianglelefteq\pi_1(\Gamma,v)$ for the normal subgroup generated by lassos on $k$-narrow-on-$J$ closed walks. $R\subseteq[M]$ is **$(f,c)$-enumerable** if $\lvert\pi_R(C)\rvert\le f(b)\,b^{cM}$; these are the coordinates on which an algorithm can afford to enumerate.

Three remarks fix the quantifiers.

1. **$k$ must be absolute.** If $k$ may depend on $b$, then $k=b$ makes the statement vacuous. The count of $k$-boxes is $\binom bk^M\le N^k$, so an absolute $k$ gives polynomially many boxes and $k=k(b)$ only gives $N^{k(b)}$: XP.
2. **$b$ bounded is trivial.** $\lvert C\rvert\le(b!)^M$ is $N^{O(1)}$ with exponent $\log_bb!$. Enumerable with $c$ absolute means the problem is only meaningful with $b,M\to\infty$ jointly. Small cases are exact facts about small graphs and say nothing about $k$ absolute.
3. **"Normally generated" versus "generated".** w27 §2.5 asks for a generating family; §7 for normal generation. They differ (§7).

**Conjecture W($k,c,s,g,f$).** For every $b,M$, $C\le S_b^M$, $C$-invariant $\Gamma$ and component $X$ there is an $(f,c)$-enumerable $R$ such that $\pi_1(X)$ is normally generated by closed walks that are $k$-narrow outside $R$, except for $\le g(b)$ further closed walks and except for $\le s(b)$ coordinates per walk.

Lead 1 is the claim that some absolute $k,c$ and some $s,g,f$ work. **Theorem B refutes this.** Conjecture W only makes the "walks are narrow" form precise; it does not by itself give FPT, and §6 explains what else is needed.

## 2. Positive theorem

**Theorem A [PROVEN].** Let $N_1,\dots,N_M\le S_b$ be 2-transitive, $C=\prod_jN_j$ on $[b]^M$, and $\Gamma$ a $C$-invariant graph. Then for every component, $\pi_1$ is normally generated by closed walks with at most $\min(b,4)$ values per coordinate.

*Proof.* If $b\le3$ nothing to do. Let $b\ge 4$. A closed walk $W=(v_0,\dots,v_L)$; fix $j$. If $W$ moves coordinate $j$, the values at $j$ form a cyclic sequence of **runs** of equal value. (Here $r\ge2$ runs.) Adjacent runs have different values, so this is a proper colouring of a cycle.

*Move.* Let a run occupy positions $s,\dots,t$ with value $c$, previous value $a$, next value $d$, and let $c'\notin\{a,d\}$. Let $W'$ be $W$ with the run recoloured $c\to c'$, i.e. with $v_i$ replaced by $v_i':=v_i[j\mapsto c']$ for $s\le i\le t$. All edges of $W'$ exist: use elements of $N_j$ fixing $a$ (resp. $d$) and mapping $c\to c'$ for the two boundary edges, and any element mapping $c\to c'$ on interior edges. 

For each $i\in[s,t]$ choose a neighbour $w_i$ of $v_i$ with $w_i(j)=e\notin\{c,c'\}$, the same $e$ for all $i$. (Such a neighbour exists: $v_i$ has an edge moving $j$ because $C$ is transitive on $V$ and $W$ moves $j$; apply an element of the stabiliser of $c$ in $N_j$ to adjust the value; $b\ge3$.) An element of $N_j$ fixing $e$ and mapping $c\to c'$ shows $w_i$ is also adjacent to $v_i'$.

Then $W$ and $W'$ differ, in $\pi_1$, by the product of lassos on these closed walks:
* the left end $v_{s-1},v_s,w_s,v_s',v_{s-1}$, 
* the prism $v_i,v_{i+1},w_{i+1},v'_{i+1},v'_i,w_i,v_i$ for $s\le i<t$, 
* the right end, symmetric to the left.

Each uses values $\{a,c,c',e\}$ or $\{c,c',e,d\}$ at $j$, and at any other coordinate at most 4 values (the values of $v_i,v_{i+1},w_i,w_{i+1}$, resp. $\le3$ at the ends). So all are 4-narrow.

*Finish.* Recolour at coordinate $j$: for each run with value outside $\{0,1,2\}$, change it to a value in $\{0,1,2\}$ differing from its two neighbours. Do this for all $j$ in turn; a recolouring at $j$ changes only coordinate $j$. The result is a closed walk with $\le3$ values per coordinate, itself $3$-narrow. $\square$

**Theorem A′ [PROVEN].** Let $C\le S_b^M$, $J\subseteq[M]$, and for $j\in J$ let $N_j:=C\cap S_b^{(j)}$ (elements moving only coordinate $j$) be 2-transitive. Let $X$ be a component of a $C$-invariant $\Gamma$ such that for each $j\in J$, either coordinate $j$ is constant on $X$ or every vertex of $X$ has an edge moving $j$. Then $\pi_1(X)$ is normally generated by closed walks that are 4-narrow on $J$ (no restriction on $[M]\setminus J$).

*Proof.* Same; the hypothesis replaces transitivity of $C$ in the choice of $w_i$. $\square$

Note that for $C$ containing $A_b^M$ the code-defined subgroups $\{g: (\mathrm{sgn}\,g_j)_j\in\mathcal D\}$ of direction 3 are covered, since $N_j\ge A_b$. So "CFI-style on coordinates" does nothing against Theorem A′ when the local kernels are large.

**Empirics (E1):** 2-transitive $H$ ($S_4,S_5,A_4,A_5,\mathrm{AGL}(1,5)$), $M\le3$: the least $k$ at homology level is $3$ on all 42 (graph, prime) pairs over $F_2$ and $F_3$ (seed 1). So the constant 4 is probably not tight; I did not prove 3.

**Why the conjecture needed independent kernels.** The run move uses only $N_j$. Without 2-transitive kernels the move is unavailable, and the failure below is genuine.

## 3. Counterexample family

**Construction.** Let $H\le S_b$ and $\Gamma_1$ be a connected $H$-invariant graph on $[b]$, non-bipartite, vertex-transitive, of odd girth $g$. Take $F\subseteq2^{[M]}\setminus\{\varnothing\}$ and define $\Gamma_{F}$ on $[b]^M$: $u\sim v$ iff the set $I$ of coordinates where they differ lies in $F$ and $u_j\sim_{\Gamma_1}v_j$ for $j\in I$. It is $C$-invariant, $C=H^M$. Let $\mathcal D:=\mathrm{span}_{F_2}\{1_I:I\in F\}\le F_2^M$, with minimum distance $d$ and dimension $\kappa$.

**Parity map.** For a closed walk $W$, $r_j(W):=$ (number of steps moving coordinate $j$) mod 2 and $r=(r_j)_j$. Each step has $r$-vector $1_I$, so $r(W)\in\mathcal D$. This defines a homomorphism $r:\pi_1(\Gamma_F,v)\to\mathcal D$. Moreover $r(tW)=r(W)$ for $t\in C$: $C$ acts trivially on $\mathcal D$.

**Theorem B [PROVEN].** If $W$ has fewer than $g$ values at coordinate $j$ then $r_j(W)=0$. The map $r$ is onto $\mathcal D$. Consequently every set of closed walks normally generating $\pi_1(\Gamma_F)$ contains at least $\kappa$ walks with $r\ne 0$, and every such walk has at least $g$ values on at least $d$ coordinates.

*Proof.* The sequence of values at coordinate $j$, with stationary steps removed, is a closed walk in $\Gamma_1$ of length $\equiv r_j(W)$. An odd closed walk contains an odd cycle, which has $\ge g$ distinct vertices. For surjectivity: given $I\in F$, take odd $L$ large enough that every vertex of $\Gamma_1$ has a closed walk of length exactly $L$; take the walk with pattern $I$ repeated $L$ times, with each coordinate $j\in I$ traversing such a closed walk. Its $r$ is $L\cdot1_I=1_I$. The kernel of $r$ contains all normal generators with $r=0$, so $\kappa$ generators with nonzero $r$ are needed to span $\mathcal D$. Each has $r\in\mathcal D\setminus0$, so weight $\ge d$. $\square$

**Instances.**
* **Folded cubes.** $n$ odd, $b=2^{n-1}$, $[b]=F_2^n/\langle1\rangle$, $\Gamma_1$ = folded $n$-cube, $H=2^{n-1}{:}S_n$ (primitive: $F_2^n/\langle1\rangle\cong$ even-weight module is irreducible for $n$ odd). Odd girth is $n$: an odd closed walk lifts to a path $x\to\bar x$ in the $n$-cube. $\lvert H\rvert=2^{n-1}n!=b^{\Theta(\log\log b)}$.
* **Odd graphs.** $\Gamma_1=O_{t+1}$ on $t$-subsets of $[2t+1]$, disjoint = adjacent, $H=S_{2t+1}$, $b=\binom{2t+1}t$, $g=2t+1$. The socle is non-abelian, so the affine structure of the folded cube is not what matters. $\lvert H\rvert=(2t+1)!=b^{\Theta(\log\log b)}$.
* Both: $g\sim\log_2b$.

**Corollary B.1 [PROVEN]: Conjecture W fails.** Fix absolute $k,c$ and any $s,g,f$. Take $b=2^{n-1}$ with $n$ odd, $n>k$, and $\log_2n$ large relative to $c$. Let $\mathcal D$ be a code of rate $1/2$, relative distance $\delta\approx0.11$ (Gilbert–Varshamov) on $M$ coordinates, $F$ the supports of a basis, $M\to\infty$. Any $(f,c)$-enumerable $R$ satisfies $\lvert R\rvert\le(cM\log b+\log f(b))/\log\lvert H\rvert=O(M/\log\log b)$ for large $M$, so $R<\delta M/2$ when $b$ is large enough, and every walk with $r\ne0$ has $\ge n>k$ values on $\ge\delta M/2$ coordinates outside $R$. Since $\kappa=M/2$ exceeds $g(b)$ for $M$ large and $\delta M/2>s(b)$, Conjecture W fails. $\square$

*Remarks.*
1. For $C\le S_b^M\times S_b^M$ and bipartite $\Gamma\subseteq V\times V'$ (the HT2 setting): take the bipartite double cover with $C$ diagonal. Closed walks have even length; the even-length parity subcode $\mathcal D_0\le\mathcal D$ has codimension $\le1$ and Theorem B applies to it with $\kappa-1$, $d$. **[PROVEN]**
2. Odd-characteristic analogue: $\mathrm{Cay}(F_p^{n-1},\{\pm e_i\})$ with $\sum e_i=0$ has a $Z_p$-valued obstruction by the same argument. **[SKETCH]**
3. $k$ between $g$ and $b$ is not excluded. A family with absolute $k$ on $b$ larger than $k$ cannot exist below $k\approx g$, and $g\sim\log b$ is what the two instances give. I do not know a primitive $H$ with large order whose invariant graph has odd girth $\gg\log b$. **[SPECULATION]** imprimitive $H$ have this trivially (e.g. $S_s\wr D_L$ on a blow-up $C_L[\overline K_s]$); that is the same phenomenon with refined coordinates.

**Other local obstruction.** $Z_2$-covers not tied to length parity (folded Johnson $F(2t,t)$, $H=S_{2t}$, cover $J(2t,t)$) also obstruct: a closed walk with nontrivial deck value has length $\ge t$ and at least $(t+1)/2$ vertices **[PROVEN]**. But they give only single-coordinate obstructions (Johnson graphs contain triangles, so parity does not couple coordinates). E2: corank 1 for walks of length $\le 4$ over $F_2$ at $t=5,6$, corank 0 over $F_3$.

## 4. Exact structure on folded cubes

**Theorem B′ [PROVEN].** Let $n$ be odd, $\Gamma_F$ on $[2^{n-1}]^M$ as above, and $F$ with $(1_I)_{I\in F}$ independent over $F_2$. Then for $4\le k<n$, $\ker r=K^k_{[M]}$: $\pi_1/K^k_{[M]}\cong\mathcal D$ and $\pi_1$ is normally generated by closed walks of length at most 4 (commutators, rectangles, cross elements, all $\le4$-narrow) together with $\kappa$ walks of length $n$.

*Proof.* $\Gamma_F$ is the Cayley graph of $A=T^M$, $T=F_2^n/\langle1\rangle$, on generators $S_F=\bigsqcup_{I\in F}S^I$, $S=\{\bar e_1,\dots,\bar e_n\}$. The free product of $Z_2$'s on $S_F$ surjects to $A$; its kernel modulo the normal closure of commutators is $\mathcal C:=\ker(F_2^{S_F}\to A)$. Commutators are closed 4-walks with at most 4 values per coordinate. So $\pi_1/\langle\langle\text{commutators}\rangle\rangle\cong\mathcal C$, and $r$ factors through $\mathcal C$. For $c\in\mathcal C$ let $\mu_j(c):=\sum_sc_se_{s_j}\in F_2^n$, the direction marginal. The condition $c\in\mathcal C$ is $\mu_j(c)\in\{0,1^n\}$ for all $j$, and $r_j(c)=\varepsilon(\mu_j(c))$, so $\ker r|_{\mathcal C}=\mathcal C_0:=\{c:\mu_j(c)=0\ \forall j\}$.

Write $A_0=F_2[S]$ with augmentation ideal $\Delta$. In $\bigotimes_{j\in I}A_0$ the **rectangles** (same $I$; a $2\times2$ pattern in two coordinates $j,l$, rest fixed) span $R_I=\sum_{j<l}\Delta_j\Delta_l A_0^{\otimes\text{rest}}$, and the quotient has dimension $1+\lvert I\rvert(n-1)$, matching the image of the marginal map on $\bigotimes_{j\in I}A_0$; so rectangles span the kernel of the marginal map within one pattern. Across patterns, the kernel of $\bigoplus_I(F_2\oplus\bigoplus_{j\in I}\Delta_j)\to\bigoplus_j A_0$ has two parts: $(\lambda_I)$ with $\sum_{I\ni j}\lambda_I=0$ for all $j$, which vanishes when the $1_I$ are independent, and tuples $(\delta_{I,j})$ with $\sum_{I\ni j}\delta_{I,j}=0$, spanned by the **cross elements** (patterns $I\ne I'$, sharing $j$; generators $s,s^*\in S^I$, $t,t^*\in S^{I'}$ agreeing off $j$ with directions $a,a'$ at $j$). Hence $\mathcal C_0$ is spanned by rectangles and cross elements, each the image of a closed 4-walk with $\le 4$ values per coordinate, so $\ker r\subseteq K^4$. Conversely closed walks with fewer than $n$ values per coordinate have $r=0$ (Theorem B), so $K^k\subseteq\ker r$ for $k<n$. The $\kappa$ length-$n$ walks with $r=1_I$ map onto a basis. $\square$

E5 confirms this at the code level for independent $F$; E2 confirms it at the graph level ($n=5$, $M=2$: corank of the $\le4$-walk span equals $\mathrm{rk}\,\mathcal D$).

## 5. Orbit sizes: walks and vectors

The algorithmic currency is the size of a $C$-invariant family, not support size per se.

**Prop O1 [PROVEN].** Take the Theorem-B family with $\Gamma_1$ the folded cube. If $W$ is a closed walk with $r_j(W)\ne0$ then its $j$th projection has direction marginal $1^n$, so it uses every direction, and its pointwise stabiliser in $H$ is trivial (an element fixing $x$ and $x+e_i$ fixes direction $i$; all directions fixed forces the identity, as $n\ge3$). So the $C$-orbit of $W$ (as a sequence) has size $\ge\lvert H\rvert^{\lvert\mathrm{supp}\,r(W)\rvert}$; up to cyclic rotation and reversal it has size at least $\lvert H\rvert^{d}/(2L)$ for $L$ the length. Hence any $C$-invariant family of closed walks that normally generates $\pi_1$ has total size (walks $\times$ length) at least $\lvert H\rvert^{d}/2=N^{\Omega((d/M)\log\log b)}$.

So no $C$-invariant family of closed walks normally generating $\pi_1$ has size $f(b)N^{O(1)}$ when $d=\Omega(M)$ and $b\to\infty$.

**Prop O2 [PROVEN].** Let $z\in Z(\Gamma_F;F_2)$ be a cycle vector with $r(z)\ne0$ and stabiliser $E\le C$. Set $r_j(z):=\lvert z\cap E_j\rvert\bmod2$, where $E_j$ is the set of edges moving $j$. Then the projection of $z\cap E_j$ to $\Gamma_1$ is a cycle $z_j\in Z(\Gamma_1;F_2)$ with $\lvert z_j\rvert\equiv r_j(z)$, invariant under $\pi_j(E)$. Thus $\lvert C:E\rvert\ge\prod_{j\in\mathrm{supp}\,r(z)}\lvert H:\pi_j(E)\rvert\ge m_H^{\,d}$, where $m_H:=\min\{\lvert H:\mathrm{Stab}_H(z)\rvert: z\in Z(\Gamma_1;F_2),\ \lvert z\rvert\text{ odd}\}$. For the folded cube, $\mathrm{Stab}(z)\cap T$ has order $\le2$, so $m_H\ge b/2$ and any invariant spanning set of the cycle space has size $\ge(b/2)^d$.

So over $F_2$ the vector form of the question ("$W^\perp$ has a small $C$-invariant spanning set") also fails to be polynomial with a small exponent, but the proven bound is only $N^{\Omega(d/M)}$: polynomial, with the exponent growing only with $d/M$.

**Data on $m_H$ (E3, exact for $n=5$).** The maximum stabiliser of an odd cycle has order 10 in $\lvert H\rvert=1920$ (exhaustive subgroup climb over good subgroups, $m_H=192$). For $n=7$ odd cycles are fixed by subgroups including $D_7$ (order 14) and a vertex-fixing $7{:}6$ (order 42), but not by $S_6,A_6,S_a\times S_b$, $A_7$, $S_7$ (structured tests only, not exhaustive). For $n=9$: $D_9$ and $3\wr3$ (order 81), not $S_8,A_8,A_9,S_9$. **[SPECULATION]** $\lvert E\rvert\le2^{O(n)}$, which would give $m_H=b^{\Theta(\log\log b)}$ and a superpolynomial vector bound. Not proved.

## 6. What the counterexample does and does not give

**Prop E (trivial quotient action) [PROVEN].** Let $X$ be a component, $A$ an abelian group, $x,y\in A^{E(X)}$ gains, $\rho_x,\rho_y:H_1(X;Z)\to A$ their classes, and $B\le H_1(X;Z)$ a $C$-invariant subgroup on whose quotient $C$ acts trivially, $Q:=H_1/B$. Write $C'$ for the stabiliser in $C$ of $\rho_x|_B$ and $S:=\{t:(\rho_x\circ t-\rho_y)|_B=0\}$ (a coset of $C'$ or empty). Then $\delta:C'\to\mathrm{Hom}(Q,A)$, $\delta(t):=\rho_x\circ t-\rho_x$, is a homomorphism, and the solutions to $\rho_x\circ t=\rho_y$ are empty or a coset of $\ker\delta$ inside $S$.

*Proof.* $\delta(t)$ vanishes on $B$. $\delta(ts)=\rho_x\circ t\circ s-\rho_x=\delta(t)\circ s+\delta(s)=\delta(t)+\delta(s)$ since $s$ acts trivially on $Q$. For $t_0\in S$, $\rho_x\circ t-\rho_y$ on $Q$ is a translate of $\delta$. $\square$

**Cor E.1 [PROVEN mod S-W objects].** On the family of Theorem B′ with abelian gains, the isomorphism test takes $f(b)N^{O(1)}$: let $\mathcal W$ be the $C$-invariant polynomial-size family of closed walks of length $\le4$; by Theorem B′ these generate $B$ with $H_1/B\cong\mathcal D$. Form the string $(\langle x,W\rangle)_{W\in\mathcal W}$; computing its isomorphisms under $C$ is object isomorphism (w27 §2.5's S-W `theo:obj`, cost $2^{O(b)}\mathrm{poly}$). Then compute $\ker\delta$ from generators by linear algebra over $A$, with $Q\cong F_2^\kappa$ given. The family is not an abelian-coefficient hard candidate.

**Prop E is stated for abelian $A$.** In the non-abelian case I only have a [SKETCH]: $t\mapsto(\rho_x\circ t)\rho_x^{-1}$ on a suitable section is a homomorphism into a product of centralisers, and the same argument applies when $C$ acts trivially on $Q$ and $Q$ has bounded rank; for rank $\kappa\sim M$, centralisers can be non-abelian and I did not complete it.

**Survival.** Against §2.5's table and Theorems G, G+, C, Prop N: the family has non-soluble surviving top $H^M$, $H$ non-enumerable, no small pinning set (Γ has small degree, a dominating set of row blocks has size $N^{1-o(1)}$), so Theorems G, G+, Reduction 0 and Theorem C do not apply. Prop N is unaffected (it only excludes Aut-reflecting reductions). So the family survives all listed routes, but Cor E.1 shows that the abelian-coefficient problem is easy by the "$C$ acts trivially on $Q$" mechanism, which the table did not list. Non-abelian gains on this family are open.

## 7. Repaired conjecture, and what this means

The refutation identifies two independent defects in the proposal: (i) wide closed walks can be forced; (ii) "forced" does not make them expensive.

**Conjecture W\* [SPECULATION].** For $C\le S_b^M$ and $C$-invariant $\Gamma$, there is a $C$-invariant family $\mathcal W$ of closed walks of size $f(b)N^{O(1)}$ with normal closure $K$, such that the $C$-action on $\pi_1/K$ is *tame*: $\pi_1/K$ is a quotient of $\bigoplus_jQ_j$, with $Q_j$ a module on which $C$ acts through its projection $\pi_j(C)$.

Tame quotients are handled coordinate-wise: the stabiliser problem on a vector in $\bigoplus_jQ_j^*$ is $C\cap\prod_j\mathrm{Stab}_{\pi_j(C)}(q_j)$, a product-type coset intersection of small orbits. **[SKETCH]** Theorem B′ is an instance with $Q_j=F_2$, $C$ acting trivially. Theorems A, A′ are instances with $Q=1$.

I have not tested W\* beyond these. In particular I have no argument for general $C$ (diagonal or wreath-coupled) and E4 shows the least $k$ for diagonal $C$ on small cases reaching $b$ (limited, and these are enumerable).

**The normal-vs-generating gap [PROVEN, elementary].** A family of closed walks that normally generates $\pi_1$ determines $\rho:\pi_1\to S_b$ only up to independent conjugacy of the values on each lasso, and complete invariants require simultaneous conjugacy of a *generating* family at one base point. (Two representations of a free group agreeing per generator up to independent conjugacy need not be simultaneously conjugate.) So "π₁ normally generated by narrow walks" does not by itself give an equivariant object for non-abelian gains. For abelian gains it does. w27 §2.5's claim of a "$T$-equivariant generating family" needs the base-point transport as well, and I have no equivariant transport. This is a gap in the strategy independent of Theorem B, and it applies even under Theorem A.

**Separately.**
* *This proof strategy:* lead 1 as stated is false. Walk-based narrow generation holds exactly when coordinate kernels are 2-transitive (Theorem A′), and it is not sufficient for non-abelian gains even there.
* *Height-2 posets:* unchanged, open. Theorem A gives a clean new narrow-generation statement; it does not give an algorithm. The family of Theorem B survives the listed routes but its abelian version is FPT, so it is not yet a hard candidate. Candidates for hardness would need non-abelian gains on non-trivial-action quotients.
* *TreeCI / R1$_c$:* no implication. Prop N still rules out Aut-reflecting reductions from R1$_c$ to height 2.
* *GI:* no implication.
