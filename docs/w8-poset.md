**Verdict.**
* *Positive.* Lead 1 of W6 works. The BCQ/ADKT antichain DP extends to every poset.
  * The cost is FPT in $b$ and the new parameter $\mu$.
  * It is uniform over antichains, chains, series compositions, unions with antichains, and
    "shallow-side" products $C_m\sqcup C_n$ with $\min(m,n)$ bounded.
  * It covers every coordinate tanglegram with bounded $\mu$, whatever leaf colouring twists it.
* *Negative, and more important for the campaign.* The coordinate-tanglegram family is **not** an
  easier sub-case of (R1).
  * With leaf colours it is R1 restricted to complete trees (R1$_c$), already for width-2 posets.
  * So no poset parameter that stays bounded on $C_h\sqcup C_h$ can give FPT without solving R1$_c$.
  * W6 §3's candidate lower-bound family (twisted coordinate tanglegrams) splits. Its bounded-$\mu$
    part is now FPT; its unbounded-$\mu$ part contains all of R1$_c$.

## 1. Setting

**Posets and fibres.**
* $P$ is a poset on $[h]$.
* $D(i)=\{j:j\prec i\}$ and $D[i]=D(i)\cup\{i\}$; $\mathrm{Up}(i)=\{k:i\prec k\}$.
* For a subset $Q$, $P|_Q$ is the induced order, and $D_Q(k)=D(k)\cap Q$.
* For a down-set $J$ and $u\in[b]^J$, the *fibre* is $\Omega_u=\{z\in[b]^h:z|_J=u\}\cong[b]^{P\setminus J}$.
  For a string $x$, $x_u(v)=x(u\cup v)$.

**Generalized wreath products** (Bailey–Praeger–Rowley–Speed 1983, "BPRS"; notation as in AMBC
§2.6, but with the order reversed: their $A(i)$ is our $D(i)$).
* For permutation groups $G_i\le S_b$, $G=\prod^{(P)}G_i$ is the set of maps $g=(\pi_i)_{i\in[h]}$,
  $\pi_i:[b]^{D(i)}\to G_i$, acting on $\Omega=[b]^h$ by $g(z)_i=\pi_i(z|_{D(i)})(z_i)$.
* $S_b^{(P)}$ is the case $G_i=S_b$.
* For every down-set $J$, the map $z\mapsto z|_J$ is $G$-equivariant, and $G$ acts on $[b]^J$ as
  $\prod^{(P|_J)}G_i$.
* **Faithful domain.** $V_P=\bigsqcup_i[b]^{D[i]}$. $G$ acts on it faithfully, $\lvert V_P\rvert\le h\,b^{\nu+1}$
  with $\nu=\max_i\lvert D(i)\rvert$, and each piece $[b]^{D[i]}$ is a union of $G$-orbits.
* **Poset block structures** (AMBC §2.5, Prop. 2.7, from BPRS). The partitions
  $F_J=\{\text{agree on }J\}$, for $J$ a down-set, form a poset block structure: uniform, pairwise
  commuting, closed under $\wedge,\vee$, distributive. Its automorphism group is $S_b^{(P)}$.
  Conversely, every poset block structure arises this way, with sets $\Omega_i$ of varying sizes
  $n_i$.

**Problem.** Given $(P,(G_i),x,y)$, with the $G_i$ as explicit subgroups of $S_b$ and $x,y:\Omega\to\Sigma$, compute
$\mathrm{Iso}_G(x,y)=\{g\in G:y(g(z))=x(z)\ \forall z\}$, a coset of $\mathrm{Aut}_G(x)$ or $\varnothing$.

**Coordinate tanglegrams.**
* $T_\sigma$ is the complete $b$-ary tree on $[b]^h$ that splits coordinates in the order $\sigma$.
* **Prop C** (W6) says that $\mathrm{Aut}(T_\sigma)\cap\mathrm{Aut}(T_\rho)=S_b^{(\sigma\cap\rho)}$.
  This is AMBC Thm 4.24(a), applied to the two linear orders, plus
  $\mathrm{Aut}(T_\sigma)=S_b^{(\text{chain }\sigma)}$.
* More generally, for $k$ trees $T_{\sigma_1},\dots,T_{\sigma_k}$ the common group is
  $S_b^{(\bigcap\sigma_j)}$ (AMBC Cor. 4.25). So posets of dimension $\le k$ are exactly the groups
  of "coordinate $k$-tanglegrams".
* So coloured coordinate tanglegram isomorphism, for a fixed pair of trees, **is** SI under
  $S_b^{(P)}$ with $P$ 2-dimensional. **[PROVEN]**

## 2. Theorem 1: the down-set DP

### 2.1 The fibre lemma **[PROVEN]**

Let $Q$ be a poset, $i\in\min Q$, $Q'=Q-i$, $U=\mathrm{Up}_Q(i)$ and $R=Q'\setminus U$ (the
elements of $Q'$ incomparable to $i$). Write $G^{(Q)}=\prod^{(Q)}G_k$.

**Lemma 2.1.**
1. $R$ is a down-set of $Q'$, and $D_{Q'}(k)=D_Q(k)\subseteq R$ for every $k\in R$.
2. Let $\rho_R:G^{(Q')}\to G^{(R)}$ be the action on the quotient $[b]^R$. Write each
   $\varphi\in G^{(Q)}$ as $\varphi(a,v)=(\pi(a),\varphi_a(v))$ for $a\in[b]$, $v\in[b]^{Q'}$. This
   gives a bijection
   $$G^{(Q)}\ \longleftrightarrow\ \{(\pi,(\varphi_a)_{a\in[b]})\ :\ \pi\in G_i,\ \varphi_a\in G^{(Q')},\ \rho_R(\varphi_0)=\dots=\rho_R(\varphi_{b-1})\}.$$

*Proof.*
1. Let $k\in R$ and $j\prec k$ with $j\in Q'$. If $j\succ i$, then $k\succ i$, which is false. And
   $j\ne i$. So $j\in R$. No element of $Q$ lies below $i$, so $D_{Q'}(k)=D_Q(k)$.
2. The parameter of $\varphi$ at $i$ is constant, since $D_Q(i)=\varnothing$; call it $\pi$.
   * For $k\in U$, the parameter of $\varphi_a$ at $k$ is $w\mapsto\pi_k(a,w)$, and these are
     independent across $a$.
   * For $k\in R$, the parameter is $w\mapsto\pi_k(w)$, the same for every $a$. By part 1 this is
     exactly the data of $\rho_R(\varphi_a)$.
   * Conversely, a tuple with equal $R$-parts defines the parameters of a unique $\varphi$. ∎

**Lemma 2.2 (recurrence).** Let $F(x,y)=\mathrm{Iso}_{G^{(Q)}}(x,y)$ for strings on $[b]^Q$, and let
$x_a=x(a,\cdot)$. Then
$$F(x,y)=\bigcup_{\pi\in G_i}\Bigl\{(\pi,(\varphi_a)):\ \varphi_a\in F'(x_a,y_{\pi a}),\ \rho_R(\varphi_a)=\psi\ \forall a,\ \text{for some}\ \psi\in\textstyle\bigcap_a\rho_R\bigl(F'(x_a,y_{\pi a})\bigr)\Bigr\},$$
where $F'=\mathrm{Iso}_{G^{(Q')}}$.

*Proof.* $y(\varphi(a,v))=x(a,v)$ for all $v$ iff $\varphi_a\in F'(x_a,y_{\pi a})$. Then apply
Lemma 2.1. ∎

**Coset bookkeeping.**
* Each $F'(x_a,y_{\pi a})$ is empty or a coset $A_af_a$, where $A_a=\mathrm{Aut}(x_a)$ (right cosets,
  right action, as in `gi`).
* So $X_a=\rho_R(A_af_a)$ is a coset in $G^{(R)}$, and $Y=\bigcap_aX_a$ is a coset $C\,y$, with
  $C=\bigcap_a\rho_R(A_a)$ depending only on $x$.
* The branch $\pi$ is then $K_0\cdot r_\pi$, where:
  * $r_\pi=(\pi,(\alpha_af_a)_a)$, with $\alpha_a\in A_a$ a lift of $y\,\rho_R(f_a)^{-1}$;
  * $K_0$ is generated by the assembled lifts $(\mathrm{id},(\mathrm{lift}_a(c))_a)$ for
    $c\in\mathrm{gens}(C)$, together with the kernels $\ker(\rho_R|_{A_a})$ placed in a single
    coordinate $a$.
* The union over $\pi$ is the coset $\langle K_0,\ r_\pi r_{\pi_0}^{-1}\rangle\cdot r_{\pi_0}$.

### 2.2 Theorem 1

Fix a linear extension $\lambda=(i_1,\dots,i_h)$ of $P$. Put $J_t=\{i_1..i_t\}$ and
$Q_t=P\setminus J_t$, and let $R_t$ be the set $R$ of Lemma 2.1 for $(Q_t,i_{t+1})$. Define
$$\mu_\lambda(P)=\max_t\ \max_{k\in R_t}\lvert D(k)\cap Q_t\rvert,\qquad\mu(P)=\min_\lambda\mu_\lambda(P).$$
By Lemma 2.1(1), $\lvert D_{R_t}(k)\rvert=\lvert D(k)\cap Q_t\rvert$. So $G^{(R_t)}$ acts faithfully on
$V_{R_t}=\bigsqcup_{k\in R_t}[b]^{D_{R_t}[k]}$, which has $\le h\,b^{\mu+1}$ points in invariant
classes of size $\le b^{\mu+1}$.

**Theorem 1.** **[PROVEN mod ADKT Thm 5]**

Given $P$, $(G_i\le S_b)$, $x,y:[b]^h\to\Sigma$ and $\lambda$ with $\mu_\lambda=\mu$, the coset
$\mathrm{Iso}_G(x,y)$ is computable using:
* at most $\tfrac43N^2\cdot b!\cdot(b-1)$ coset intersections of two cosets of subgroups of
  $G^{(R_t)}$ on $V_{R_t}$;
* plus $N^{O(1)}$ further polynomial-time group operations.

With Arvind–Das–Köbler–Toda's coset intersection (FSTTCS 2010, Thm 5: $2^{O(k)}n^{O(1)}$ for groups
with a common invariant partition into classes of size $\le k$) the total is
$$b!\cdot2^{O(b^{\mu+1})}\cdot N^{O(1)}.$$
An optimal $\lambda$ is found in $O(2^hh^2)\le O(Nh^2)$ time by a DP over down-sets. So SI under
generalized wreath products is **FPT in $(b,\mu(P))$**, and for $\mu=0$ it costs
$b!\,2^{O(b)}N^{O(1)}$.

*Proof.*
* **Table.** The DP table is $F_t(u,u')=\mathrm{Iso}_{G^{(Q_t)}}(x_u,y_{u'})$ for $u,u'\in[b]^{J_t}$,
  and the answer is $F_0(\varnothing,\varnothing)$.
* **Base and step.** At $t=h$ the group is trivial and the entry tests $x(u)=y(u')$. Lemma 2.2
  computes $F_t$ from $F_{t+1}$, using the bookkeeping of §2.1.
* **States.** There are $\sum_{t\le h}b^{2t}\le\tfrac43N^2$ states (since $b\ge2$). Each has
  $\le\lvert G_i\rvert\le b!$ branches. Each branch makes $b-1$ pairwise intersections of the $X_a$ in
  $G^{(R_t)}$.
* **Invariant classes.** The groups $\rho_R(A_a)$ and the representatives $\rho_R(f_a)$ lie in
  $G^{(R_t)}$, which preserves each piece $[b]^{D_{R_t}[k]}$ of $V_{R_t}$. These pieces are the
  invariant classes ADKT needs, of size $b^{\lvert D_{R_t}(k)\rvert+1}\le b^{\mu+1}$.
* **Other operations.** Projection is restriction to $V_{R_t}\subseteq V_{Q_{t+1}}$ (a union of
  pieces, by Lemma 2.1(1)). Lifting is a preimage under that restriction (Schreier–Sims on
  $V_{Q_{t+1}}$ with $V_{R_t}$ as base prefix). Assembly writes down an element of $G^{(Q_t)}$ on
  $V_{Q_t}$. All three are polynomial in $\lvert V_{Q_t}\rvert\le hN/b^t$. Summed over states this is
  $\sum_tb^{2t}\mathrm{poly}(hN/b^t)=N^{O(1)}$.
* **Optimal $\lambda$.** The DP over down-sets $J$ is
  $\mathrm{best}(J)=\min_{i\in\min(P\setminus J)}\max(\mathrm{cost}(J,i),\mathrm{best}(J+i))$. There
  are $\le2^h$ down-sets. ∎

**Remarks.**
1. *Other coset-intersection engines.* All subgroups involved lie in $\Gamma_b$, so Luks's
   $n^{O(b)}$ bound gives $(h\,b^{\mu+1})^{O(b)}$, which is FPT because $(\log N)^{O(b)}\le f(b)N$.
   Babai's quasipolynomial CI gives $\exp(O((\mu\log b+\log h)^c))$. None of these is needed when
   $b\le4$: then everything is solvable and W6 Prop L already gives polynomial time.
2. *Special cases.*
   * **Antichain.** $R_t=Q_t-i_{t+1}$, $G^{(R)}=S_b^{R}$ on $R\times[b]$. This is ADKT's Theorem 6
     DP (coloured hypergraphs with colour classes $\{i\}\times[b]$), and BCQ's DP without the top
     group.
   * **Chain.** $R_t=\varnothing$, there is no intersection, and the DP is AHU on pairs of subtrees.
   * Every intermediate poset interpolates between the two.
3. *Why it works.* By Lemma 2.1, branching on a **minimal** $i$ decouples the up-set $U$ completely
   across the $b$ sub-fibres. What remains coupled is only the quotient action on $[b]^R$. That group
   has a small faithful domain when $\mu$ is small, *even though $[b]^R$ itself can have size close
   to $N$*. W6 §5 lead 1 proposed branching only on minimal elements; this is that idea, with the
   coupling made exact.
4. *Mixed sizes.* The proof never uses equal coordinate sizes. Coordinates $i$ with
   $\lvert\Omega_i\rvert=n_i\le b$ and $G_i\le\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega_i)$ work verbatim, with $N=\prod n_i$.
   Prop B needs this.
5. *Not done.* Canonical forms (as opposed to isomorphism cosets). Groups that also permute
   coordinates (subgroups of $S_b^{(P)}\rtimes\mathrm{Aut}(P)$, like BCQ's top group) would need
   BCQ's DP over pairs of down-sets $(J,J')$, which I did not work out. Coordinate tanglegrams do not
   need either, since tree levels are fixed.

### 2.3 Subgroups

**Corollary 1.1.** **[PROVEN mod ADKT Thm 5]**
1. For $H\le G$ given by generators,
   $\mathrm{Iso}_H(x,y)=\mathrm{Iso}_G(x,y)\cap H$. That is one more coset intersection on $V_P$, with
   classes $[b]^{D[i]}$ of size $\le b^{\nu+1}$. The total is $b!\,2^{O(b^{\nu+1})}N^{O(1)}$, FPT in
   $(b,\nu)$, and $\mu\le\nu$.
2. If $H=\mathrm{Aut}_G(c)$ for colourings $c$ of $\Omega$ (or of the pieces of $V_P$, pulled back to
   $\Omega$), then $\mathrm{Iso}_H(x,y)=\mathrm{Iso}_G((x,c),(y,c))$, and no extra intersection is
   needed.
3. *Necessity.* For a chain $P=C_h$, item 1 with arbitrary $H\le W_h$ is TreeSI on complete trees
   (W2's group core). So for generator-given subgroups, some bound that is unbounded on chains, such
   as $\nu$, is needed unless TreeCI is FPT. This is the definable/generator gap (R2) of T2,
   unchanged.

### 2.4 The parameter $\mu$ **[PROVEN]** (the values for $m\le6$ were also computed, E4)

* $\mu\le\nu\le h-1$.
* $\mu=0$ for antichains, chains and the "N" poset. $\mu$ is 0 exactly when some linear extension
  never leaves an element incomparable to the current one with an unprocessed predecessor.
* $\mu(P\oplus Q)=\max(\mu(P),\mu(Q))$, where $\oplus$ is the ordered sum ("nesting"): every linear
  extension processes $P$ first.
* $\mu(P\sqcup A)\le\mu(P)$ for an antichain $A$: process $P$ first, and $A$ only adds
  predecessor-free elements to each $R_t$.
* $\mu(C_m\sqcup C_n)=\min(m,n)-1$. Upper bound: process the longer chain first. Lower bound: the
  first step leaves the whole other chain in $R_1$.
* $\mu\bigl((A_m{<}k)\sqcup(A_m{<}k)\bigr)=m$, where $A_m{<}k$ is an antichain of $m$ elements
  below a top $k$. At the first step the other $k$ has all $m$ predecessors unprocessed. This poset
  is 2-dimensional (§4).
* $\mu$ of the standard example $S_m$ is $m-1$ (computed for $m\le6$).
* **Random 2-dimensional posets** $P_{\mathrm{id},\rho}$ (E4; 200 samples per $h$, 60 for $h\ge16$):

| $h$ | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 | 14 | 16 | 18 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| distribution of $\mu$ | 0:143 1:55 2:2 | 0:77 1:113 2:10 | 0:32 1:129 2:36 3:3 | 0:9 1:96 2:78 3:17 | 0:1 1:63 2:100 3:33 4:3 | 1:10 2:31 3:15 4:4 | 1:1 2:20 3:28 4:9 5:2 |
| mean $\mu_\sigma$ (trivial order $\sigma$) | 1.43 | 2.46 | 3.99 | 5.11 | 6.79 | 7.73 | 10.05 |
| mean $\nu$ | 3.3 | 4.9 | 6.6 | 7.9 | 9.9 | 11.6 | 13.5 |
| mean width / height | 3.1/3.2 | 3.6/3.8 | 4.4/4.3 | 5.0/4.7 | 5.5/5.3 | 5.6/5.9 | 6.2/6.2 |

So the optimal order matters (mean 3 vs 10 at $h=18$), and $\mu$ is much smaller than width,
height or $\nu$ on typical coordinate tanglegrams. How $\mu$ grows asymptotically on random
2-dimensional posets is not determined.

## 3. Prop H: the width-2 collapse

$W_h$ denotes the automorphism group of the complete $b$-ary tree of depth $h$, and
$P_h=C_h\sqcup C_h$ two incomparable chains. **(R1$_c$)** is coloured tanglegram isomorphism
restricted to two *complete* $b$-ary trees of equal depth. Node colours can be pushed to leaf
colours (each leaf records its ancestors' colours by depth), so only leaf colours matter.

**Proposition H.**
1. $S_b^{(P_h)}=W_h\times W_h$ in product action on $[b]^h\times[b]^h$. So SI under $S_b^{(P_h)}$ is
   exactly $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ restricted to complete trees. It is also the coloured coordinate
   tanglegram $(T_\sigma,T_\rho)$ with $\sigma=(C^1,C^2)$, $\rho=(C^2,C^1)$. **[PROVEN]**
2. R1$_c$ $\le$ SI under $S_b^{(P_h)}$, with $N\mapsto N^2$. **[PROVEN]**
3. For every poset $P$ on $h'$ points, SI under $S_b^{(P)}$ $\le$ R1$_c$, with $N\mapsto O((bh'N)^2)$.
   **[PROVEN mod T2 Theorem A]**
4. *Induced subposets.* If $P'$ is an induced subposet of $P$, then SI under $S_b^{(P')}$ on $N'$
   points reduces to SI under $S_b^{(P)}$ on $N'b^{\lvert P\rvert-\lvert P'\rvert}$ points. The
   reduction preserves the decision, and $\mathrm{Aut}$ restricts to one fibre. **[PROVEN]**

So **SI under generalized wreath products over all posets $\equiv$ R1$_c$ $\equiv$ the width-2
case $C_h\sqcup C_h$ $\equiv$ coloured coordinate tanglegrams**, all with polynomial blow-up.

*Proof.*
1. $D(i)$ is the part of $i$'s own chain below it, so $g$ acts on each $[b]^{C^j}$ by a tree
   automorphism, independently of the other chain. Prop C with $\sigma\cap\rho=C^1\sqcup C^2$.
2. Identify both trees with the standard complete tree; the leaf bijection is $\varphi$ and the leaf
   colouring $c$. Set $M(\ell,\ell')=c(\ell)$ if $\ell'=\varphi(\ell)$, and $\bot$ otherwise. Then
   $(\alpha,\beta)\in W_h\times W_h$ maps $M_1$ to $M_2$ iff $\beta\varphi_1=\varphi_2\alpha$ and
   $c_2\alpha=c_1$, i.e. iff it is a tanglegram isomorphism. This is T2 Thm A(ii).
3. Take a realizer $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_k$ of $P$ ($k=\dim P\le h'$).
   * By AMBC Cor. 4.25, $\bigcap_j\mathrm{Aut}(T_{\sigma_j})=S_b^{(P)}$.
   * Encode this as an HS$(b,2)$ structure: $k$ sorts, each a copy of $[b]^{h'}$ carrying
     $T_{\sigma_j}$; identity relations between sort 1 and sort $j$; and $x$ on sort 1.
   * Apply T2 Thm A(v) (sorts hung under a complete $b$-ary top of depth $\lceil\log_bk\rceil$, padded
     with dummy subtrees of a distinct colour) and A(i) ($T^*\ltimes T^*$).
   * All trees stay complete and $b$-ary, so the output is an R1$_c$ instance on
     $(b^{\lceil\log_bk\rceil}N)^2\le(bh'N)^2$ leaves.
4. Colour $z$ by $(x'(z|_{P'}),\,z|_{P\setminus P'})$, and likewise $y$.
   * Isomorphisms then fix all coordinates outside $P'$.
   * On each fibre $z|_{P\setminus P'}=v$ they act by some $\varphi_v\in S_b^{(P')}$ (induced
     subposet: $D(i)\cap P'=D_{P'}(i)$), and each $\varphi_v$ must lie in $\mathrm{Iso}(x',y')$.
   * Conversely, any $\varphi\in\mathrm{Iso}(x',y')$, applied on every fibre, is in
     $\mathrm{Iso}(x,y)$. ∎

**Corollaries.** **[PROVEN]**
* **H.1.** Let $\kappa$ be any poset parameter that is bounded on $\{C_h\sqcup C_h\}$; width,
  dimension and the number of chains are examples. Then SI under $S_b^{(P)}$ is FPT in $(b,\kappa)$
  iff R1$_c$ is FPT.
* **H.2.** A poset class containing, for every $m$, some $P$ with an induced $C_m\sqcup C_m$ and
  $\lvert P\rvert=O(m)$ is R1$_c$-hard, by items 2 and 4.
* **H.3.** Theorem 1 is consistent with this. $\mu(C_m\sqcup C_n)=\min(m,n)-1$, and it reproduces
  T2's "FPT when one tree is shallow" for complete trees.
* **H.4.** Every $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ / tanglegram algorithm question for complete trees is a
  question about generalized wreath products, and conversely.

*Check (E3).* Complete-tree tanglegrams $(T_{\rm id},T_\rho\circ\varphi)$ were encoded as strings on
$[b]^{2h}$ under $S_b^{(C_h\sqcup C_h)}$, with $\varphi$ the identity, 1 or 2 transpositions, or
random. Theorem 1's DP ($\mu=h-1$) gives the same $\lvert\mathrm{Aut}\rvert$ as nauty on the original
tanglegram in **32/32** cases ($b=2,h\le4$; $b=3,h=2$).

## 4. Coordinate tanglegrams (task 2)

1. **Uncoloured** coordinate tanglegrams are trivial. The group is $S_b^{(P_{\sigma,\rho})}$ in closed
   form (Prop C / AMBC 4.24), and the plain tanglegram has no further data. **[PROVEN]**
2. **Coloured** coordinate tanglegrams (the "twisted by leaf colourings" families of W6 §3) are FPT
   in $(b,\mu(P_{\sigma,\rho}))$ by Theorem 1. **[PROVEN mod ADKT]**
   * For $b\le4$ they are polynomial for every $P$ (W6 Prop L).
   * W6's E1 instances ($h\le6$) all have $\mu\le1$.
3. **Every coordinate tanglegram FPT?** With colours this is **equivalent to R1$_c$ FPT**. One
   direction is Prop H.2 (width-2 posets $C_h\sqcup C_h$ suffice). The other: coloured coordinate
   tanglegrams *are* R1$_c$ instances. **[PROVEN]** So the answer is "yes iff complete-tree
   tanglegram isomorphism is FPT". Unconditionally, it is yes for bounded $\mu$, and that covers
   most small random coordinate structures (E4).
4. **Near-coordinate tanglegrams.**
   * *Leaf-colour twists* of $(T_\sigma,T_\rho)$, by any colouring, are covered by item 2 with the
     same $\mu$. E2/E2b test sums mod $b$, differences of incomparable coordinates, quadratic forms
     along the poset relations or across incomparable pairs, a third tree's level-2 nodes, sparse
     marks and random colourings. All agree with nauty.
   * *Bijection twists* $(T_\sigma,T_\rho\circ\varphi)$ are arbitrary complete-tree tanglegrams,
     i.e. R1$_c$. By Prop H they are coloured coordinate tanglegrams over $C_h\sqcup C_h$ on $N^2$
     points with $\mu=h-1$, so Theorem 1 gives only $f(b,h)$. That is XP, as it must be unless R1$_c$
     is FPT.
   * If $\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(T_\sigma)\,\mathrm{Aut}(T_\rho)$ (one double coset), the twist is
     invisible. E6 hit this case once: a sibling transposition.
5. **Coordinate $k$-tanglegrams** ($k$ complete trees on one leaf set) have groups $S_b^{(P)}$ with
   $\dim P\le k$ (AMBC 4.25). Theorem 1 covers them with the same parameter. **[PROVEN mod ADKT]**
6. **Consequence for W6 §3.** The candidate lower-bound family "coordinate tanglegrams with twisted
   leaf colourings" splits. The bounded-$\mu$ part is FPT. The unbounded-$\mu$ part contains (for
   $P=C_h\sqcup C_h$) all of R1$_c$, so as a hardness candidate it is no more specific than R1$_c$
   itself. A seventh necessary condition for hard instances, added to W6 §3's list: unbounded $\mu$
   of every invariant poset block structure (§5.2).

## 5. Relation to the general case (task 3)

### 5.1 Prop B: recognising coordinate structure

Let $T,T'$ be complete $b$-ary trees of depth $h$ on a common leaf set $L$. Let $E_j$ and $E'_j$ be
their level-$j$ partitions, and $\mathcal L=\mathcal L(T,T')$ the sublattice of the partition
lattice of $L$ generated by them under $\wedge,\vee$.

**Proposition B.**
1. $\mathrm{Aut}(T)\cap\mathrm{Aut}(T')=\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal L)$, the permutations preserving every
   partition in $\mathcal L$. **[PROVEN]**
2. Suppose $\mathcal L$ is a poset block structure (uniform, pairwise commuting, distributive). Then:
   * $L\cong\prod_{m\in M}\Omega_m$ for the poset $M$ of join-irreducibles of $\mathcal L$, with
     $2\le\lvert\Omega_m\rvert\le b$;
   * $\mathrm{Aut}(T)\cap\mathrm{Aut}(T')=\prod^{(M)}\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega_m)$;
   * coloured isomorphism for this pair of trees is SI under that group, FPT in $(b,\mu(M))$ by
     Theorem 1;
   * if all $\lvert\Omega_m\rvert=b$, then $(T,T')\cong(T_\sigma,T_\rho)$ with $M=\sigma\cap\rho$ (up to
     AMBC's reversed convention).

   **[PROVEN mod BPRS/AMBC's representation theorem and Prop. 2.7]**
3. Conversely, $\mathcal L(T_\sigma,T_\rho)=\{F_J:J\text{ a down-set of }P_{\sigma,\rho}\}$. **[PROVEN]**
4. $\mathcal L$, and the test in item 2, are computable in time polynomial in $\lvert L\rvert$. If
   $\mathcal L$ is a poset block structure, it has $\le2^{\lvert M\rvert}\le\lvert L\rvert$ elements, so the
   closure can stop as soon as it exceeds $\lvert L\rvert+1$ elements. **[PROVEN]**

*Proof.*
1. $G=\mathrm{Aut}(T)\cap\mathrm{Aut}(T')$ maps blocks to blocks for every generator, hence for their
   meets and joins. Conversely, $T$ is determined by its level partitions (nodes = blocks,
   edges = containment), so $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal L)\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}(T)$; likewise for $T'$.
2. The representation theorem (AMBC §2.5) gives $L\cong\prod\Omega_m$ with
   $\mathcal L=\{\Pi_D\}$, and Prop. 2.7 gives the automorphism group. For the bound on
   $n_m=\lvert\Omega_m\rvert$:
   * In AMBC's convention $\Pi_{D_1\cap D_2}=\Pi_{D_1}\wedge\Pi_{D_2}$. Write $E_j=\Pi_{X_j}$ and
     $E'_k=\Pi_{Y_k}$ for decreasing chains of down-sets.
   * By distributivity, every element of $\mathcal L$ is a union of sets $X_j\cap Y_k$. So the
     principal down-set $D[m]$ equals $X_j\cap Y_k$ for some $j,k$. Take $j$ maximal; then
     $X_{j+1}\cap Y_k\subseteq D(m)$, the unique lower cover.
   * Hence $n_m=[\Pi_{D[m]}:\Pi_{D(m)}]\le[E_j\wedge E'_k:E_{j+1}\wedge E'_k]\le[E_j:E_{j+1}]=b$,
     because a block $C\cap C'$ splits into at most $b$ blocks $C_s\cap C'$.
   * If every $n_m=b$, each level step $X_j\supsetneq X_{j+1}$ removes exactly one element of $M$.
     This defines $\sigma$, and $T\cong T_\sigma$; likewise $T'\cong T_\rho$.
3. Meets and joins of the partitions $F_A$ correspond to unions and intersections of the coordinate
   sets $A$. The lattice generated by prefixes of $\sigma$ and of $\rho$ consists of the unions of
   sets $\sigma[1..j]\cap\rho[1..k]$, which are exactly the down-sets of $P_{\sigma,\rho}$.
4. Immediate. ∎

*Check (E6).* 36 complete-tree tanglegrams with $(b,h)\in\{(2,4),(2,5),(3,3),(2,6)\}$:
* All 12 coordinate tanglegrams generate a poset block structure, with
  $\lvert\mathcal L\rvert=\#\text{down-sets}(P_{\mathrm{id},\rho})$ and exactly $h$
  join-irreducibles.
* Of the 24 twisted ones (one transposition, or a random bijection), 23 do not. Either they contain
  non-uniform partitions, or the closure exceeds $\lvert L\rvert+1$.
* The exception is a sibling transposition in $T_\rho$. It is an automorphism of $T_\rho$, so that
  tanglegram is still coordinate.

### 5.2 Decomposition into coordinate pieces plus twisting

1. **Trivial decomposition** (Prop H.2). Every coloured complete-tree tanglegram is a coloured
   coordinate tanglegram over $C_h\sqcup C_h$: all the twisting moves into a permutation-matrix
   colouring. This decomposition always exists, but it puts everything into the twist.
   **[PROVEN]**
2. **Intrinsic decomposition** (Prop B). The coordinate part is the poset block structure generated
   by the instance's own invariant partitions. When it exists, its poset can be far richer than
   $C_h\sqcup C_h$: for example an antichain (digit reversal, $\mu=0$), where Prop H's encoding has
   $\mu=h-1$.
   * One can add any further $G$-invariant partitions that are computable isomorphism-invariantly
     (jointly for both instances), e.g. from 2-WL classes, to the generators before closing. **[PROVEN]** for the correctness of using any invariant
     partitions; whether this ever helps on hard instances: **[SPECULATION]**.
   * "Twisting" then means precisely the failure of the generated lattice to be a poset block
     structure with small $\mu$.
3. **Relation to Prop S** (W6's straddling recursion). Theorem 1 *is* Prop S, run along the
   $G$-invariant tree $T_\lambda$ (any linear extension of $P$ gives one).
   * At the node $(J_t,u)$ the straddling structure is the quotient $[b]^{R_t}$, and the fibre product
     over it is Lemma 2.1.
   * Prop S failed for general tanglegrams because the straddling combine is TreeCI on the
     straddling leaves, which are not few.
   * Here the straddling leaves $[b]^{R_t}$ are just as many, but the straddling **group**
     $G^{(R_t)}$ has a faithful domain of size $h\,b^{\mu+1}$ with bounded invariant classes. The
     gain comes from a small faithful domain, not from a small straddling set.
   * For general tanglegrams this suggests a criterion: FPT whenever the straddling projections
     $\rho_i(G^{\rm loc}(u_i))$ have faithful domains of size $f(b)\,\mathrm{poly}(\log n)$ with
     bounded invariant classes. But that is a property of the answer, not of the input, the same
     obstacle as in Prop T. **[SPECULATION]**
4. **General (non-complete) trees.** Prop D reduces to prime instances. I found no reduction from R1
   to R1$_c$: unbalanced trees cannot be padded to complete ones in FPT size. **Open.**

### 5.3 The exact remaining hard case

**(RH)** SI under $W_h\times W_h$ in product action on $[b]^h\times[b]^h$, equivalently
$T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ on complete trees, equivalently R1$_c$.

By Prop H, any FPT algorithm for (RH) gives FPT for all generalized wreath products, all coordinate
tanglegrams, and all complete-tree tanglegrams. Hard instances of (RH) must satisfy all of:
* $b\ge5$, with deep non-solvable towers on both sides (W6 Prop L);
* unbounded twisting $\tau$ at many scales (T2 Prop T);
* both depths unbounded: $\mu(C_{h_1}\sqcup C_{h_2})=\min-1$, by Theorem 1;
* no invariant poset block structure with bounded $\mu$ (Prop B plus Theorem 1);
* prime (W6 Prop D).

Two concrete open sub-questions:
1. **Height-2 posets with large $\mu$.** For example $(A_m{<}k)\sqcup(A_m{<}k)$, which is
   2-dimensional and has $\mu=m$, but no induced $C_3\sqcup C_3$, so Prop H.2 does not apply.
   * Its group is $(S_b\wr S_b^{m})\times(S_b\wr S_b^{m})$: product-action tops with a small
     faithful domain, over "gauge" bases with orbits of size $b$.
   * If it is FPT, $\mu$ is not the right parameter, and the true dividing line would be about long
     incomparable chains. If it is R1$_c$-hard, $\mu$ is essentially right.
   * I could do neither. **[open]**
2. **R1 vs R1$_c$**, i.e. whether unbalanced trees add difficulty. **[open]**

## 6. Experiments (`docs/w8-poset-code/`)

The implementation (`w8lib.py`):
* represents elements as permutations of $[b]^{Q_t}$;
* uses $[b]^{R}$ rather than $V_R$ as the coset-intersection domain (simpler, and correct, but
  larger);
* intersects cosets by backtracking over base images, with pruning by the second group's chain
  (`find_rep`), and intersects groups Butler-style (`group_intersection`);
* lifts and computes kernels through `gi.group.ActionHom`.

in $G$ and maps $x$ to $y$; and the order and the isomorphism decision agree with brute force or

**Assessment.**
* **Theorem 1.** No algorithm for SI, coset intersection or canonization under generalized wreath
  products over a non-trivial poset was found, in the sub-agent's search (about 48 tool calls) or my
  checks. It is an elementary extension of ADKT/BCQ (antichain) and AHU (chain). The only new
  ingredients are Lemma 2.1 (branch on minimal elements; the shared part is a down-set quotient) and
  the parameter $\mu$. **New as far as searched; experts may find it routine.**

* **Prop B** combines the classical representation theorem with the out-degree bound. AMBC study the
  related property that a group's invariant partitions form a poset block structure ("PB property").
  The tanglegram reading is new as far as I found.

## 8. Leads (ranked)

1. **Height-2 posets with large $\mu$** ($(A_m{<}k)\sqcup(A_m{<}k)$; then all height-2 posets).
   Try a BCQ+ADKT hybrid: the product-action top on its small domain, with the bottom "gauges"
   $\pi_k:[b]^{D(k)}\to S_b$ treated as a coset CSP with Mal'tsev structure. For a fixed top
   permutation the gauge problem is a coset intersection with orbits of size $b$, hence FPT. The open
   point is interleaving it with the top DP. This decides whether $\mu$ is the right parameter
   (§5.3).
2. **Theorem 1 × Prop T.** Split each shared group $G^{(R_t)}$ into a small-domain part, intersected
   directly, and a "definable" part with bounded Goursat quotient, solved by tuple colourings. This
   would interpolate between $\mu$ and $\tau$. It works cleanly only when $U$ and $R$ are incomparable
   (a direct product); the semidirect case loses Goursat normality.
3. **Poset-block-structure refinement as preprocessing** for R1$_c$ / tanglegrams. Close the trees'
   level partitions together with WL-derived invariant partitions under $\wedge,\vee$, and if a poset
   block structure with small $\mu$ appears, run Theorem 1. A first test: Prop H's $C_h\sqcup C_h$
   encodings of *coordinate* tanglegrams should collapse back to $P_{\sigma,\rho}$.
4. **R1 vs R1$_c$.** Either reduce general trees to complete ones (heavy-path or centroid hierarchies
   with colours), or find a structural reason why unbalanced trees are easier (Prop S's smaller-half
   recurrence is polynomial when the combine is cheap).
5. **Engineering.** Make Theorem 1 a dispatcher case in `gi`. Recognise a generalized wreath product
   from its invariant partitions (AMBC's PB property), then run the DP with $V_R$ domains and ADKT- or
   Luks-style intersection. Coordinate tanglegrams and colour-refinement-resistant tanglegrams (T2
   E5) are natural benchmarks.
6. Canonical forms for Theorem 1, and the coordinate-permuting extension ($S_b^{(P)}\rtimes\mathrm{Aut}(P)$).
