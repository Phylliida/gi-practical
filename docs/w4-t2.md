**Verdict.**
* *Algorithm.* I did not find an FPT algorithm for full-group $T^{\,2}$. The problem is more robust
  than it looked. It is the same problem as tanglegram isomorphism, as GI of hierarchically clustered
  graphs, and as CI of definable tree groups. Its own block DP reproduces it at the same size at
  every node. WL, IR and rank-logic methods fail on it already at $b=2$.
* *What is new* (as far as my searches found; all elementary). The tanglegram / FullCI / HCGI
  equivalences. The precise location of the

  embedding into binary tanglegrams. The $\tau$-parameterized algorithm. Non-2-closed definable

* *The diagonal shape is not where the difficulty lies.* With definable data it *is*

## 1. Setting, and two corrections to the brief

**Trees and groups.** As in W2 §1. All trees are rooted with out-degree $\le b$ and may carry node
colours. $W(T)=\mathrm{Aut}(T)$ is the group of colour-preserving automorphisms, acting faithfully on
the leaves $L(T)$. A **full** (coloured) tree group is one of the form $\mathrm{Aut}(T,x)$ for a
colouring $x$ of the nodes: an iterated product of wreath products of symmetric groups, and 2-closed.
Uncoloured complete trees with leaf-pair colourings are equivalent to this: pad with dummy leaves and
push node colours down to the leaf matrix, as W2 §6.1 does.

$T^{\,2}_{\rm full}(b)$: given coloured trees $T_1,T_2$ and $M,M':L_1\times L_2\to\Sigma$, compute
$\mathrm{Iso}_{W_1\times W_2}(M,M')$, where $(\alpha,\beta)$ maps $M$ to $M'$ iff
$M'(\alpha r,\beta c)=M(r,c)$ for all $r,c$.

**Correction 1 (WL dimension).** The brief says that WL dimension $f(b)$ on tree-product graphs would
"settle this case". It would not. $k$-WL costs $n^{k+1}$, so dimension $f(b)$ gives $N^{O(f(b))}$:
XP, which is already known (Luks gives $N^{O(b)}$, GNS $N^{\mathrm{polylog}\,b}$). Only a dimension
*independent of $b$* would give FPT, indeed polynomial time. §5 shows the dimension is unbounded
already at $b=2$, so neither happens.

**Correction 2 (what "full group" covers).** W2 Prop 6.1 colours every node pair. So the
"full-group" case already includes $\mathrm{Aut}$ of *coloured* trees, rigid tops, and arbitrary
leaf-pair data. This is the class that matters, and it is much richer than "$W\times W$ with random
data" suggests (§2, §5).

## 2. The graph core: equivalences

### 2.1 Definitions

* **Tanglegram** (Matsen–Billey–Kas–Konvalinka arXiv:1507.04784; Konvalinka–Wagner
  arXiv:1512.01168): two rooted trees $T,T'$ whose leaf sets are identified by a bijection. As a
  graph, it is the union of the two trees glued along the leaves, with the two roots, and the nodes
  of the two trees, coloured apart. An isomorphism is a graph isomorphism that respects these colours
  and the leaf colours, if any.
  * A tanglegram with out-degree $\le b$ has maximum degree $b+1$.
  * Its automorphism group is $\mathrm{Aut}(T)\cap\mathrm{Aut}(T')$ on the common leaf set.
  * Isomorphism types are the double cosets $\mathrm{Aut}(T)\backslash S_n/\mathrm{Aut}(T')$
    (Konvalinka–Wagner).
* **FullCI$(b)$**: given full coloured tree groups $A=\mathrm{Aut}(T,x)$, $A'=\mathrm{Aut}(T',y)$ on a
  common leaf set and $a,c\in\mathrm{Sym}(L)$, compute $Aa\cap A'c$.
* **HCGI$(b)$**: given a vertex- and edge-coloured graph $X$, with no degree bound, and a coloured
  rooted tree $T$ of out-degree $\le b$ with $L(T)=V(X)$, compute the isomorphisms that preserve both
  $X$ and $T$. Equivalently, SI for $W(T)$ acting diagonally on $L\times L$; this is W2's
  $P_{\rm graph}$ after pushing node-pair colours to leaf pairs.
* **HS$(b,r)$** (hierarchically sorted structures): a fixed finite set of sorts, each carrying a
  coloured rooted tree of out-degree $\le b$, plus coloured relations of arity $\le r$ on the leaves
  (across sorts allowed). Isomorphisms are tuples of tree isomorphisms preserving the relations.
* **Definability.** $G\le W(T)$ is *defined* by an HS structure $Y$ if one sort of $Y$ is $T$ and
  $\mathrm{Aut}(Y)$ restricted to that sort is exactly $G$. A family of tree groups is
  **fpt-definable** if, from generators of $G$, a defining HS$(f(b),2)$ structure of size
  $f(b)\,\mathrm{poly}(N)$ is computable in that time.
* **Def-CI$(b)$**: given two HS$(b,2)$ structures $Y_1,Y_2$ sharing the sort $T$, and $a,c$, compute
  $G_1a\cap G_2c$, where $G_i$ is the group defined by $Y_i$. The structures are the input, not
  generators.

### 2.2 Theorem A (the graph core) **[PROVEN]**

The following are fpt-equivalent, by reductions polynomial in size that keep the parameter $b$, up
to $b\mapsto\max(b,O(1))$:

$$T^{\,2}_{\rm full}\ \equiv\ \text{GI}(\text{coloured }T\,\square\,T)\ \equiv\ \text{Tanglegram-Iso}\ \equiv\ \text{FullCI}\ \equiv\ \text{HCGI}\ \equiv\ \text{HS}(b,r)\text{-GI}\ \ (r\text{ fixed})\ \equiv\ \text{Def-CI}.$$

Moreover $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}(b)\le$ coloured GI of maximum degree $b+1$ (via tanglegrams), improving
W2's $2b+2$.

*Proof.* The non-trivial step is (i).

**(i) $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}\le$ Tanglegram.** Let $T_1\ltimes T_2$ be $T_1$ with a copy of $T_2$ hung,
root merged, at every leaf of $T_1$. Its leaves are $L_1\times L_2$, its out-degree is $\le b$, and
its depth is $h_1+h_2$. Define $T_2\ltimes T_1$ symmetrically.

*Key lemma:* $W(T_1\ltimes T_2)\cap W(T_2\ltimes T_1)=W_1\times W_2$ on $L_1\times L_2$.
* The merge nodes of $T_1\ltimes T_2$ (the former leaves of $T_1$, marked by a colour) have leaf
  sets the rows $\{l_1\}\times L_2$. The merge nodes of $T_2\ltimes T_1$ have leaf sets the columns.
  So an element $g$ of the intersection permutes rows (by some $\alpha$) and columns (by some
  $\beta$).
* Since $(l_1,l_2)$ is the unique point of its row and its column, $g=(\alpha,\beta)$.
* $\alpha$ is $g$'s action on the top part of $T_1\ltimes T_2$, which is $T_1$, so
  $\alpha\in W_1$. Likewise $\beta\in W_2$.
* Conversely, $(\alpha,\beta)$ acts on $T_1\ltimes T_2$ by $\alpha$ on top and $\beta$ on every
  copy simultaneously. Node colours are carried along.

So the coloured tanglegram $(T_1\ltimes T_2,\ T_2\ltimes T_1)$ with leaf colours $M$ has automorphism
group $\mathrm{Aut}_{W_1\times W_2}(M)$, and isomorphisms correspond likewise.

*Check.* E1 confirms this on 54 random and structured instances. A hand check shows that the
interleaved trees $T_1\otimes T_2$, $T_2\otimes T_1$ would **not** do: their intersection contains
independent row swaps inside different $2\times2$ blocks (§6, remark).

**(ii) Tanglegram $\le T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$:** take $M$ to be the permutation matrix of the leaf
identification. **(iii) Tanglegram $\equiv$ FullCI:** $g\in\mathrm{Aut}(T,x)a\cap\mathrm{Aut}(T',y)c$
iff $g$ is an isomorphism from the tanglegram $((T,x),(T',y))$ to $((T,x)^a,(T',y)^c)$. **(iv)**
$T\,\square\,T\equiv T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ is W2 Prop 6.1, which holds for arbitrary rooted trees with
depth colours.

**(v) HS$(b,r)\le T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$.**
* *Several sorts:* hang the sort trees under a new root, with children coloured by sort.
* *A binary relation $R$ on one tree $T^*$:* use the instance $(T^*,T^*,M)$ with
  $M(x,y)=(R(x,y),[x=y])$. The identity block forces $\alpha=\beta$, so
  $\mathrm{Aut}=\{(\alpha,\alpha):\alpha\in W(T^*)\cap\mathrm{Aut}(R)\}$.
* *Arity $r>2$:* add a sort for $L_2\times\cdots\times L_r$, carrying the tree
  $T_2\ltimes\cdots\ltimes T_r$, and binary "projection" relations $\pi_j(z,(l_2,\dots,l_r))\iff z=l_j$
  to each sort $j$. These force every copy of $T_j$ inside the product tree to move
  exactly as sort $j$ does: for $r=3$, $[\gamma z=\beta_{l_2}(l_3)]=[z=l_3]$ for all $z,l_3$ forces
  $\beta_{l_2}=\gamma$. The $r$-ary relation then becomes binary between $L_1$ and the product sort.
  The size is $N^{r-1}$.

  In particular tensors under $W_1\times\cdots\times W_r$ ($T^{\,r}_{\rm full}$) reduce to $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$:
  **arity collapses at 2**.

**(vi) HCGI $\equiv T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$.** HCGI is HS$(b,2)$ with one sort. Conversely, a $T^{\,2}$
instance is an edge-coloured bipartite graph on $L_1\sqcup L_2$ clustered by $T_1\sqcup T_2$ under a
new root.

**(vii) Def-CI $\equiv$ HS-GI.**
* *$\le$, gluing:* $G_1a\cap G_2c=\mathrm{Iso}(Y_1\cup_TY_2,\ Y_1^a\cup_TY_2^c)|_T$. Here $Y_1^a$
  relabels only the sort $T$, and $\cup_T$ shares the sort $T$ while keeping the other sorts
  disjoint and coloured by origin. An isomorphism of the union is a pair of isomorphisms that agree
  on $T$.
* *$\ge$:* FullCI is the special case in which $Y_1,Y_2$ are the two coloured trees themselves
  (a full coloured group is defined by its own tree). With (i)–(iii) and (v) this closes the cycle. ∎

**Corollary A.1 (W2 Question 6.2 restated).** W2 asked whether $P_{\rm graph}(b)$ is fpt-equivalent
to TreeCI$(b)$. By Theorem A, $P_{\rm graph}\equiv T^{\,2}_{\rm full}\equiv$ Def-CI, so the question
is exactly whether TreeCI reduces to its definable restriction. **[PROVEN]** (It also settles that
W2's diagonal form $P_{\rm graph}$ and the $W\times W$ form are equivalent, by (v) and (vi).)

### 2.3 Where TreeCI sits **[PROVEN]**

**Proposition A′.** TreeCI$(b)\equiv_{\rm fpt}$ CI$(G,\ \mathrm{Aut}(T,x))$, with $G\le W(T)$ arbitrary
(given by generators) and $\mathrm{Aut}(T,x)$ a full coloured group *on the same tree*.

*Proof.* TreeSI$(G,x,y)=G\cap\mathrm{Iso}_{W(T)}(x,y)$, and the second factor is a coset of
$\mathrm{Aut}(T,x)$, computable in polynomial time by coloured-tree canonical forms. TreeSI $\equiv$
TreeCI is W2 Thm 2.1. ∎

So the whole campaign core sits in one table:

| problem | the two groups | status |
|---|---|---|
| SI for $W(T)$ on $L$ | full, full, same tree | P (AHU) |
| $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ = tanglegram = HCGI = Def-CI | **full, full, different trees** (or definable, definable) | open; the graph core |
| TreeSI = TreeCI | **arbitrary, full, same tree** | open; the group core |

Since $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ is a sub-problem of TreeCI and of degree-$d$ GI, the gap between
$T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ and TreeCI is exactly **(R2) definability**: TreeCI $\le_{\rm fpt}T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$
if every tree group is fpt-definable.

### 2.4 Relation to degree-$d$ GI

**Proposition A″.** **[PROVEN, elementary]**
1. $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}(b)\le$ coloured GI of maximum degree $b+1$ (via tanglegrams).
2. Degree-$d$ GI $\equiv$ isomorphism of depth-2 tanglegrams whose leaf-parents have out-degree
   $\le d$ and whose roots have **unbounded** degree. The forward map: subdivide, so the input is
   bipartite $(A,B,E)$ with sides fixed; let the leaves be $E$, tree 1 be root $\to a\in A\to$ edges
   at $a$, and tree 2 be root $\to b\in B\to$ edges at $b$. Isomorphisms correspond, because a leaf
   $e=ab$ lies under $a$ in tree 1 and under $b$ in tree 2.
3. Tanglegram isomorphism with unbounded out-degree is GI-complete already at depth 2, by item 2.

So $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ and degree-$d$ GI differ *only* in whether the hierarchy above the vertices is
$b$-ary. HCGI says the same thing more strongly: given an invariant $b$-ary hierarchy, even **dense**
graphs reduce to bounded-degree GI.

**Corollary A.2.** **[CONDITIONAL: (R2), and W2 §3.5's coordinate-tree residual gap closed, i.e.
CI for coordinate trees $\le_{\rm fpt}$ TreeCI]** Degree-$d$ GI is FPT iff coloured tanglegram
isomorphism is FPT in out-degree. *Reason:* GI$_{\deg}\le$ SI$(\hat\Gamma_d)\equiv$ CI(coordinate
trees) (Luks; W2 Prop 3.2 **[SKETCH]**) $\le$ TreeCI (by the hypothesis) $\le T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ (by
(R2)) $\le$ GI$_{\deg}$ (Prop A″.1). Unconditionally, only the necessary direction holds:
**GI$_{\deg}$ FPT $\Rightarrow$ tanglegram isomorphism FPT.**

## 3. The diagonal-on-direct-factor shape **[PROVEN]**

**The shape** (RT §3.3). A node $B$ of the interleaved tree $T_1\otimes T_2$ with children
$C_i=(u_i,v)$. Here $H_{C_i}=A_i\times G_2$ with $A_i\le W(u_i)$ and $G_2\le W(v)$, the kernel is
$K=\prod_iA_i\times\mathrm{diag}(G_2)$, and child cosets $X_i\subseteq H_{C_i}$ are given. The
problem DS is to compute $K\cap\prod_iX_i$.

**Proposition D.**
1. *Arbitrary data.* If the $X_i$ are arbitrary cosets, DS is TreeCI-complete, even with all ambient
   groups full. Take $A_i$ trivial (the $u_i$ leaves) and two children: then
   $K\cap(X_1\times X_2)=\{(g,g):g\in X_1\cap X_2\}$ is an arbitrary CI in $W(v)$ (this is
   PCI$_{\rm diag}$, W2 Thm 2.1(c)).
2. *Definable data.* Suppose $A_i$ and $G_2$ are defined by HS structures (full groups are
   defined by their trees), and each $X_i$ is the isomorphism coset of a sub-instance (a submatrix
   with its defining structures), as in any DP run on an HS / $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ instance. Then DS
   reduces to one $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ instance of the same size: glue the defining structures to
   the two sorts and add the matrix (Theorem A (v), (vii)).
3. *No progress inside $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$.* On a $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ instance, the combine at
   $(u,v)$ is $\mathrm{Iso}$ of $M|_{L(u)\times L(v)}$ under $\prod_iW(u_i)\times W(v)$. That is the
   sub-instance itself with the children of $u$ coloured apart. Equivalently it is CI in $W(v)$ of
   the projections $\mathrm{proj}_2\,\mathrm{Iso}((u_i,v),\cdot)$, i.e. Def-CI. W2 §4.6 calls this
   the "hierarchical set-of-strings isomorphism"; it is not a simpler sub-problem but
   $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ at the same size.

**Answer to task 2.** Yes: any algorithm for $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ extends to the
$K_1\times\mathrm{diag}(G_2)$ shape whenever $K_1$ and $G_2$ are definable, which covers every shape
that the DP generates on graph-derived instances (Theorem A (vii)). The shape becomes TreeCI-hard
only through non-definable data. The diagonal coupling itself is free: it is an identity block.
**The difficulty lies in $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ itself (R1) and in definability (R2), not in the
diagonal.**

## 4. Algorithms

### 4.1 The brief's ideas, one by one

It equals Def-CI, which is $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ at the same size (Prop D.3) |
| set-of-strings via sorted canonical forms | exact when one tree has depth $\le s$: $(b!)^{b^{s}}\mathrm{poly}$ | canonical forms of rows under $W_2$ must be *simultaneous* (one shared $\beta$). That is CI again. On CFI instances (§5) every row's individual canonical form is the same |

### 4.2 Proposition T: FPT in the instance's twisting **[PROVEN]**

For $u\in T_1$ and $v\in T_2$, let $M_{u,v}=M|_{L(u)\times L(v)}$ and
$\mathrm{Aut}(M_{u,v})\le W(u)\times W(v)$. Write its Goursat data as follows:
* projections $P_1,P_2$;
* $N_2(u,v)=\{\beta:(1,\beta)\in\mathrm{Aut}\}$, which is $\mathrm{Aut}(T_2(v),c_{u,v})$, where
  $c_{u,v}$ colours each column $c\in L(v)$ by its column string in $M_{u,v}$. This is a **full
  coloured** group;
* $Q(u,v)=P_2/N_2\cong P_1/N_1$.

Define the **twisting index** $\tau(M)=\max_{u\ne\mathrm{root},\,v}\lvert Q(u,v)\rvert$, maximised
also over $M'$.

**Proposition T.** $\mathrm{Iso}_{W_1\times W_2}(M,M')$ is computable in
$b!\cdot\tau^{\,b}\cdot\mathrm{poly}(N_1N_2)$.

*Proof.*
* **Table.** DP over pairs $(u,v),(u',v')$, splitting rows first. The entry $I((u,v),(u',v'))$ is
  the set of tree isomorphisms $(\alpha,\beta)$ carrying $M_{u,v}$ to $M'_{u',v'}$. It is empty or a
  coset of $\mathrm{Aut}(M_{u,v})$.
* **Leaves of $T_1$.** For $u$ a leaf, $I$ is SI of a string under coloured-tree isomorphisms
  $T_2(v)\to T_2(v')$: polynomial (AHU canonical forms).
* **Branches.** For internal $u$ with children $u_i$, fix a bijection $\sigma$ to the children of
  $u'$ ($\le b!$ choices). Let $Y_i=\mathrm{proj}_2\,I((u_i,v),(u'_{\sigma i},v'))$, a coset
  $P_2(u_i,v)\beta_i$ or empty.
* **Transversals.** A transversal $R_i$ of $N_2(u_i,v)$ in $P_2(u_i,v)$ is the orbit of the
  colouring $c_{u_i,v}$ under the generators of $P_2(u_i,v)$, so $\lvert R_i\rvert=\lvert Q\rvert\le\tau$.
  This uses $\gamma\gamma'^{-1}\in N_2\iff c^\gamma=c^{\gamma'}$.
* **Splitting.** So $Y_i=\bigcup_{t\in R_i}N_2(u_i,v)t\beta_i$, and each piece is
  $\{\gamma:c_{u_i,v}^{\gamma}=c_{u_i,v}^{t\beta_i}\}$.
* **Intersections.** For a choice $(t_1,\dots,t_k)$, the intersection of the pieces is
  $\{\gamma:(c_{u_1,v},\dots,c_{u_k,v})^{\gamma}=(d_1,\dots,d_k)\}$. That is SI of a *tuple*
  colouring under full coloured-tree isomorphisms: polynomial, and a coset of
  $\mathrm{Aut}(T_2(v),(c_{u_i,v})_i)$.
* **Union.** $\bigcap_iY_i$ is the union of these $\le\tau^{k}$ cosets. It is the projection of an
  isomorphism set, hence a coset; generators come from the group parts plus quotients of the
  representatives.
* **Lift.** Lift each generator $\beta$ to $(\alpha_i)_i$ by a preimage computation in the coset
  $I((u_i,v),(u'_{\sigma i},v'))\subseteq\mathrm{Iso}(T_1(u_i),T_1(u'_{\sigma i}))\times\mathrm{Iso}(T_2(v),T_2(v'))$ (Schreier–Sims on the product action), and
  add the kernel $\prod_iN_1(u_i,v)$. ∎

*Remarks.*
* This is Theorem E with the ambient entanglement $\varepsilon$ replaced by the instance's own
  Goursat quotient. It works because the "inner" parts $N_2$ are full coloured groups, which can be
  intersected by canonical forms.
* Running columns-first gives the same with column strips, so the parameter can be taken as
  $\min(\tau_{\rm row},\tau_{\rm col})$.
* *Reading.* Hard instances need, at many scales, large groups of row permutations that are
  realisable **only** together with column permutations. In a tanglegram (permutation matrix) rows
  have pairwise distinct strings, so $N_1=1$ and $Q(u,\mathrm{root})\cong P_1$: $\tau$ is as large as
  the automorphism groups of the sub-tanglegrams on the $L(u)$. Tanglegrams are the purest twisted
  instances.
* On CFI-in-$T^{\,2}$ (§5) the quotients $Q$ are elementary abelian of exponential order. An FPT
  treatment of *abelian* twisting would push the residual to deep non-abelian twisting (lead 1, §7).

### 4.3 Definability: evidence for (R2)

The definable tree groups are closed under intersection (gluing) and projection (by definition),
and contain the full coloured groups. Further members:

| group | defining structure | label |
|---|---|---|
| twisted diagonal $\{(w,w^\varphi)\}$ on two full subtrees; synchronisation of the top $k$ levels of several subtrees | identity block, or the block $[\mathrm{anc}_k(c)=r]$, to a fresh row copy | **[PROVEN]** |
| level-synchronised towers $S_b^h$ in product action (STR's Theorem L family) | digit-reversal tanglegram (E5: $\lvert\mathrm{Aut}\rvert=(b!)^h$ in all 7 cases) | **[PROVEN]**, verified |
| $\mathbb Z_p$-linear "codes" of flips ($p\le b$), CFI-type | parity gadgets with auxiliary variables (as in §5) | **[PROVEN]**, standard |
| odometer $\mathbb Z_{2^h}$, regular on $L$ | successor relation plus identity block | **[PROVEN]** |
| **$W(T)\cap\mathrm{Alt}(L)$** (not 2-closed on $L$) | Prop S below | **[PROVEN]**, verified to $N=64$ |
| Example X′ / iterated X′ (W2 §4.3) with full building blocks | Cayley structure at the top ($f(b)$ size) plus diagonals | **[PROVEN]** |

**Proposition S.** For a complete binary tree $T$ with $N$ leaves, $W(T)\cap\mathrm{Alt}(L)$ is
defined by a bounded-degree coloured structure on $O(N)$ points.

*Proof.* The construction:
* At each internal node $v$, attach a parity pair $\{v^0,v^1\}$.
* At each bottom node $w$, add edges $w^i$–(the $i$-th child leaf of $w$).
* At each higher internal node $v$, with children $v_0,v_1$, add a CFI gadget $q_{i_0i_1}$
  ($i_0,i_1\in\mathbb Z_2$) adjacent to $v$, to $v^{i_0+i_1}$, to $v_0^{i_0}$ and to $v_1^{i_1}$.
  This is symmetric in $v_0\leftrightarrow v_1$.
* Colour the two root pair points apart.

Why it works:
* An automorphism acts on the pairs by flips $f_v$.
* The bottom rule forces $f_w=\sigma_w(g)$, the swap bit at $w$.
* The gadgets force $f_v=f_{v_0}+f_{v_1}$.
* By induction $f_v=\mathrm{sgn}(g|_{L(v)})$ relative to the left–right identification. Swapping
  two halves of $2^k\ge4$ leaves is even, so only bottom swaps contribute.
* The root colouring forces $\mathrm{sgn}(g)=0$.
* Conversely, every even $g$ extends uniquely.

E4 confirms $\lvert\mathrm{Aut}\rvert=\lvert W\rvert/2$, a faithful projection and even generators
for $h=2,\dots,6$, with maximum degree 9. ∎

*Why it matters.* $\mathrm{Alt}(n)$ itself is the classic group that is **not** the restriction of the
automorphism group of a bounded-arity structure with polynomially many extra points. Inside tree
groups the parity decomposes along the tree, and the obstruction disappears. So the naive
obstruction to (R2) fails. I found neither a proof of (R2) nor a candidate counterexample.
**[SPECULATION]** The likeliest counterexamples are generator-given groups with deep non-abelian
cocycles between levels, for which no local defining rule is apparent.

## 5. Lower bounds for algorithm classes

### 5.1 Theorem B: CFI inside binary tanglegrams **[PROVEN mod the standard CFI game argument]**

**Construction.**
* Let $H$ be a connected cubic graph with ordered vertices, $n=\lvert V(H)\rvert$ and $m=3n/2$.
* *Rows:* the CFI middle vertices $m_{v,A}$, for $A\subseteq E(v)$ with $\lvert A\rvert\equiv t(v)$
  (mod 2). Take $t\equiv0$ for $X_0$, and $t=\delta_{v_0}$ for the twisted $X_1$.
* *Row tree $T_1$:* a rigid binary tree with uniquely coloured nodes over the base vertices. Below
  vertex $v$ hangs a binary **gadget tree** of depth 2, splitting by $[e_1(v)\in A]$ and then by
  $[e_2(v)\in A]$, with the edges at $v$ in base order.
* *Columns:* $e^0,e^1$ for each base edge $e$, under a rigid binary tree, one binary node per edge.
* *Matrix:* $M_t(m_{v,A},e^i)=1$ iff $e\in E(v)$ and $[e\in A]=i$. This is the bipartite CFI graph
  of Neuen–Schweitzer's $R(\cdot)$ form.
* *Encodings:* $\Gamma^{\square}_t$ = the coloured $T_1\square T_2$ ($b=2$, degree $\le6$, $O(n^2)$
  vertices). $\Gamma^{\rm tg}_t$ = the **sparse tanglegram**: leaves are the incidences
  $(m,e^i)$; tree 1 is $T_1$ with a rigid binary comb of $m$'s three incidences; tree 2 is $T_2$
  with $e^i$ over its four incidences, grouped by endpoint. It has out-degree 2, is subcubic, and
  has $O(n)$ vertices (measured $\approx36n$). For experiments only, the **2-sorted** encoding is
  the two trees plus the bipartite edges $m$–$e^i$ between their leaves; it has unbounded degree.

**Theorem B.** $\Gamma_0\not\cong\Gamma_1$ for both encodings. If the robber escapes $3(k+1)$ cops on
$H$ (e.g. $3(k+1)\le\mathrm{tw}(H)$, up to an additive constant), then $k$-WL does not distinguish
$\Gamma_0$ from $\Gamma_1$.
For cubic expanders, $\mathrm{tw}(H)=\Theta(n)$. Hence:
* coloured binary tanglegrams (subcubic) have WL dimension $\Omega(\lvert V\rvert)$;
* coloured $T\,\square\,T$ of binary trees have WL dimension $\Omega(\lvert V\rvert^{1/2})$.

*Proof.*
1. *Automorphisms.* Colours fix the rigid tops, so automorphisms preserve each gadget subtree and
   each column pair. Hence $\mathrm{Aut}_{W_1\times W_2}(M_t)$ is the group of colour-preserving
   automorphisms of the CFI graph that respect the trees.
2. *Flip maps respect the trees.* For $F\subseteq E(H)$, the flip map $f_F$ swaps $e^0\leftrightarrow e^1$
   for $e\in F$ and sends $m_{v,A}\mapsto m_{v,A\triangle(F\cap E(v))}$. On gadget trees this is
   translation of the coordinate bits, so it is a tree isomorphism: the trees split by membership
   bits, and a flip XORs a fixed bit vector. So $f_F$ is an isomorphism
   $\Gamma_t\to\Gamma_{t+\partial F}$ of either encoding, where $\partial F(v)=\lvert F\cap E(v)\rvert$
   mod 2.
3. *Automorphism group.* It is the cycle space, of order $2^{m-n+1}$.
4. *Non-isomorphism.* $\Gamma_0\not\cong\Gamma_1$, because an isomorphism would be a
   colour-preserving isomorphism $X_0\to X_1$ of CFI graphs. Those do not exist for connected $H$
   (parity of $\sum t$).
5. *The game.* For the WL bound, run the standard bijective-pebble-game proof of the CFI theorem
   (CFI 1992 Thm 6.4; in the cops-and-robber form of Dawar–Richerby) on $\Gamma$ instead of $X$.
   Duplicator uses the bijections $\hat\beta_w$ = (a flip along a path from $v_0$ to the robber
   vertex $w$, then a fixed bit flip inside gadget $w$). Each is a tree-respecting bijection
   $\Gamma_0\to\Gamma_1$ that preserves all colours and edges except at vertices whose **support**
   contains $w$.
6. *Supports.* The support of a $\Gamma$-vertex is the set of base vertices of the gadget of its
   row part, together with the endpoints of the edge of its column part. It has at most 3 elements.
   Each pebble therefore occupies at most 3 cops, and the standard argument goes through verbatim
   with $3k$ cops. ∎

*Check (E2).* On $K_4$, $K_{3,3}$, the cube, Petersen and random cubic graphs with
$n=16,24,40$:
* $\lvert\mathrm{Aut}\rvert=2^{m-n+1}$ exactly, in all three encodings;
* nauty: non-isomorphic in every case;
* colour refinement: same histograms in every case;
* 2-WL: does not distinguish, on the 2-sorted encoding (up to 222 vertices) and on the tanglegram
  encoding (up to 214 vertices).

### 5.2 IR and rank logic **[SKETCH]**

* **IR.** Neuen–Schweitzer (arXiv:1705.03283, main theorem and Theorem `lower-bound-from-meager`)
  build rigid multipede graphs $R(G)$ over random bipartite bases $G=(V,W,E)$, with $V$-degree $r$.
  Their colour classes are the pairs $F(w)$ and the gadgets $M(v)$, so the base is **ordered by
  colours**, exactly as in §5.1. Every $k$-realizable IR algorithm has search trees of size
  $2^{\Omega(n)}$ on them.
  * Gadget trees of depth $r-1$ split by the membership bits of $N(v)$ in a fixed order. Any such
    lexicographic coordinate tree is invariant under the flips, as in §5.1.
  * The same encoding gives binary-tree $T^{\,2}$ / tanglegram versions $\Gamma(R(G))$. They are
    rigid, since $\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)=\mathrm{Aut}(R(G))\cap$(tree-preserving) $=1$, and their
    vertices have support $\le2$.
  * *Gap:* their argument counts $\simeq_k$-equivalent tuples in $R(G)$. Transferring it needs
    "$\simeq_{ck}$ on $R(G)$ $\Rightarrow$ $\simeq_k$ on $\Gamma$", which follows from §5.1's support
    argument, plus a check that their tree-size count survives the encoding. I did not write this out.
* **Rank logic.** Lichter (arXiv:2104.12999, JACM 2023) shows that rank logic does not define
  isomorphism of CFI structures over $\mathbb Z_{2^q}$ with **totally ordered** base graphs (of
  unbounded degree).
  * A gadget $\{\mathbf a\in\mathbb Z_{2^q}^{N(v)}:\sum\mathbf a=0\}$ carries an invariant binary tree:
    lexicographic in the first $\deg v-1$ coordinates, each coordinate refined by residues mod $2,4,\dots,2^q$
    (the odometer tree). Translations preserve it.
  * Hence these structures embed into binary $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ through an FPC-interpretation, and
    rank logic cannot define isomorphism on the image class.
  * *Gap:* uniformity of the interpretation in $q$ (the residue levels use the numeric sort) is not
    checked.

### 5.3 Comparison with known WL results

* *Background.* Bounded-degree graphs have WL dimension $\Theta(n)$ in the worst case (CFI over
  expanders; constants in Kiefer–Neuen arXiv:2402.03274, Schneider–Schweitzer arXiv:2403.12581).
  Bounded treewidth, genus or excluded minors give bounded dimension (Grohe).
* *Theorem B adds* that the very special shape "two binary trees glued along their leaves" already
  carries the worst case. Trees themselves have WL dimension 1. Searches (2026-11-05) for WL
  dimension of Cartesian products of trees and of tanglegrams found nothing. **Novelty: routine
  construction; not found stated.**
* *Upper side, experimental.* On *plain* tanglegrams (E5) colour refinement does nothing beyond depth
  classes, but 2-WL computes the orbit partition on all 21 tested. On random coloured $T^{\,2}$
  instances (E3) colour refinement already computes the orbits. So: random instances are easy,
  plain tanglegrams need dimension 2, and CFI-type instances need $\Omega(n)$.
* *Consequence.* At $b=2$ every class here is in P by group theory (the groups are 2-groups; Luks).
  These lower bounds therefore separate combinatorial from group-theoretic methods, as CFI always
  does. **Any FPT algorithm for $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$ must be group-theoretic, or at least go beyond
  WL, IR and rank logic.**

### 5.4 Hardness reductions

None found. W2 §5.1 applies verbatim:
* $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}(b)$ is in $N^{\mathrm{polylog}\,b}$ (GNS);
* so a $k$-Clique reduction would need $b\ge2^{k^{\Omega(1)}}$ under ETH.

The new reformulations suggest no source either. Tanglegram isomorphism with unbounded degree is
GI-complete (Prop A″), so the out-degree parameter plays exactly the role that maximum degree
plays for GI. A W-hardness proof for tanglegrams would be one for a parameterized GI variant, which
nobody has.

## 7. Residual problem and leads

**(R1) The graph core.** Coloured tanglegram isomorphism, equivalently $T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$, HCGI,
FullCI or Def-CI: is it FPT in the out-degree $b$?
* It is necessary for degree-$d$ GI to be FPT (Prop A″).
* By Prop T, hard instances must have large twisting $\tau$ at many scales.
* By §5, they defeat WL, IR and rank logic already at $b=2$ (via abelian twisting).
* Abelian and bounded-orbit twisting look tractable by linear algebra or ADKT (lead 1). The
  genuinely open part is **deep non-abelian twisting**: tanglegram-like coupling between two deep
  $b$-ary hierarchies whose intersection group has large $A_k$ sections ($k\le b$) at unbounded
  coupling depth.

**(R2) Definability.** Is every $b$-ary tree group, given by generators, fpt-definable by an HS
structure?
* If yes: TreeCI $\equiv$ (R1), i.e. the group core equals the graph core. With W2 §3.5 closed,
  degree-$d$ GI FPT $\Leftrightarrow$ tanglegram isomorphism FPT (Cor A.2).
* If no: degree-$d$ GI may be strictly easier than TreeCI, as W2 §6.3 speculated.
* Partial evidence for yes: §4.3, including the non-2-closed $W\cap\mathrm{Alt}$.

**Leads (ranked).**
1. **Abelian twisting.** Extend Prop T to instances where every Goursat quotient $Q(u,v)$ is abelian
   (or solvable with bounded orbits). The combine is then "find $\gamma$ in a full tree group mapping
   a tuple of colourings into a product of affine orbits", which is linear algebra over
   $\mathbb Z_p$, $p\le b$, if the orbits decompose along the tree. CANON's "abelian entanglement is
   free" (Lemma N, Theorem E-canon) is the template. This would cover every CFI-type instance and
   isolate deep non-abelian twisting as the true residual.
2. **(R2) for 2-groups.** Try to prove that all subgroups of $C_2\wr\cdots\wr C_2$ are fpt-definable,
   level by level: a submodule of the permutation module, defined by parity gadgets arranged
   invariantly, plus a cocycle realised by "parity pair" sorts as in Prop S. Prop S is the case
   of a single character. A failure would give the first natural candidate for TreeCI $\not\le T^{\,2}_{\rm full}$.
3. **Tanglegram recursion by straddling sets.** At the root of $T$, the $T'$-nodes that straddle
   two children of the root form an upward-closed subtree $S$ of $T'$. The children decouple
   except through the action on $S$, so bounded $\lvert S\rvert$ everywhere gives FPT (this is close
   to bounded treewidth). The question is whether the coupling through a large $S$ can be handled by
   recursing *into $S$* rather than enumerating it.
4. **Lower bound for group-theoretic recursion.** Plain and digit-reversal tanglegrams are the
   cleanest instance family on which to state a coset-splitting lower bound (W2 §5.3). Here the
   ambient groups are full and the only data is a permutation.
5. **Practical.** Plain tanglegrams of complete trees defeat colour refinement completely (E5). They
   are a cheap stress family for the repo's IR solver (`gi.graph_iso`, `gi.bounded`), alongside
   CFI.
